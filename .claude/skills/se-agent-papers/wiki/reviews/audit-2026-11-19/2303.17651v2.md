# Self-Refine: Iterative Refinement with Self-Feedback

Audit opened on 19 November 2026 · version `2303.17651v2`. [Paper record](../../sources/arxiv-2303.17651.md) · [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17651v2) · [Retrieval metadata](../../raw/metadata/2303.17651.metadata.json)

## Section review

- Sections 1–2, Algorithm 1, and Figures 1–3. In successive calls, the same model produces an initial response, a textual critique, and a revision. A stopping criterion or budget caps the number of iterations. This inference-time procedure requires no separately trained critic. [PDF, p. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.17651v2#page=1) [PDF, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.17651v2#page=2) [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.17651v2#page=3)
- §3 and Table 1. The authors evaluated three models on seven tasks. The 21 published differences average 18.73 points. The average combines preference rates and execution metrics and cannot be interpreted as 18.73% more correct solutions on a common sample. The three math gains were 0.0, 0.2 and 0.2 points. GPT-4 gained 49.2 points on the dialogue evaluation. [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.17651v2#page=4) [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.17651v2#page=5)
- Sections 4–7, Tables 2–3, and Figures 4–5. Removing specific feedback reduced performance on the tasks analyzed. For example, ChatGPT scored 27.5 on code optimization with the full critique and 24.8 without it. The discussion covers iteration, the knowledge needed for self-correction, and prior approaches involving separate training.
