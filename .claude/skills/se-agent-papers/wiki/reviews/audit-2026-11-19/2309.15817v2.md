§§1–3, Figures 1–5 and Tables 1–2. ToolEmu separates the agent, a model that simulates tool responses and the evaluation of safety and usefulness. The simulator permits evaluation of actions when the request and environment state are underspecified, without performing external operations. Section 4 and Tables 3–4. Each of 100 validation scenarios produced two trajectories for the same agent. External annotators judged 72.5% of the standard emulator’s failure indications correct, versus 68.8% for the challenging-scenario variant. As a share of all cases, correctly identified failures increased from 39.6% to 50.0%, a 10.4-point gain. Error margins in the tables denote one standard error. Table 4 shows that 91.9% and 85.6% of simulations, respectively, had no critical flaws. A separate test reproduced 6 of 7 selected cases for one agent and one tool set. Sections 5–7, Table 5. A safety instruction for GPT-4 reduced the rate of flagged failures from 39.4% to 23.9% and raised helpfulness from 1.458 to 1.824 on a 0–3 scale. A system taking no action had a 0% failure rate and helpfulness of 0.063. The evaluator can miss hazards, and the emulator can attribute unrealistic capabilities to tools. Appendix A and Tables A.1–5 define simulation state, rubrics and case selection. Appendix B and Tables B.1–6 cover component-level evaluation, a second annotation by the authors and variability with temperature. Appendices C–D and Tables C.1–2 describe agent requirements and the annotation procedure. Appendix E covers traces, simulator comparisons and checks of selected outcomes in a running environment.
