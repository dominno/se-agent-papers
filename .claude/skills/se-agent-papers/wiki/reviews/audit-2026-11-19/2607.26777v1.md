# CodeSpec: Dual Executable Specifications for Agentic Long-Horizon Feature Development

Audit opened on 19 November 2026 for version `2607.26777v1`, 8 pages. [Paper record](../../sources/arxiv-2607.26777.md) · [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26777v1) · [Authors, license and checksum](../../raw/metadata/2607.26777.metadata.json)

## Reading notes

- **Introduction and background.** The MLflow example shows how a locally plausible change can bypass an object factory and break a call chain (Figure 1). The review distinguishes adding a feature to an existing project from synthesizing a single function and discusses the role of textual plans in coding agents. [PDF, p. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.26777v1#page=1) [PDF, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.26777v1#page=2)
- **Method.** For each subrequirement, the agent selects code units and links neighboring units based on functional needs and existing dependencies. Figure 2 and Equations 5–8 define two specifications. One checks units, relationships and data flow. The other checks results, boundary conditions and state changes. Algorithm 1 updates the patch after feedback and stops when both sets of checks pass or the budget runs out. [PDF, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.26777v1#page=2) [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.26777v1#page=3) [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.26777v1#page=4)
- **Experimental design.** FeatureBench Lite/Fast/Full contain 30/100/200 tasks. Each task contributes its percentage of passing fail-to-pass tests to %Passed. #Resolved counts tasks passing all such tests. The authors report a bootstrap mean and standard deviation from 10,000 task resamples. [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.26777v1#page=5)
- **RQ1, Tables 1–2.** With CodeSpec, DeepSeek-V4-Pro achieves 70.7%, 55.0% and 49.9% and fully solves 9, 17 and 28 tasks on the respective subsets. GPT-5.4-mini on Lite scores 54.7% and fully solves five tasks. On Fast, Table 1 credits Mini-SWE-Agent with the most fully solved tasks (24), despite its lower %Passed (49.6%). The text’s claim that CodeSpec also leads on Fast in fully solved tasks conflicts with the table. [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.26777v1#page=5)
- **RQ2, Table 3.** On Lite, removing both specifications yields 62.6%. Removing behavioral checks, architectural checks or rationales for links yields 64.0%, 66.6% and 64.8%, respectively. The full method achieves 70.7% at $0.18 per task, compared with $0.09 without specifications. [PDF, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.26777v1#page=6)
- **RQ3, Figure 3.** With descriptions of 3,000–5,000 words, the executable plan scores 71.8% versus 43.8% for the text plan. Above 5,000 words the two scores converge to 38.3% and 36.3%, respectively. The size of the advantage depends on the task group. [PDF, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.26777v1#page=6)
- **RQ4, Table 4.** Across 104 Python repository-building tasks in NL2Repo, CodeSpec averages 46.6% and fully solves eight tasks. On Hard it achieves 20.4%, compared with 19.3% for the best baseline in that column. [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.26777v1#page=7)
- **Discussion, conclusions and final materials.** Figure 4 gives one MLflow example (100% and 56.7%). It illustrates how the difference arises but cannot establish how often it occurs. Version v1 ends with references, including both benchmarks. The authors’ code is publicly available. ## Independent check of the paper card’s findings

From the image of Table 1, the fully solved proportions on the three subsets are 9/30 = 30%, 17/100 = 17% and 28/200 = 14%. On Fast, CodeSpec leads RTADev by 5.3 percentage points on %Passed, while Mini-SWE-Agent fully solves seven more tasks than CodeSpec. Both differences follow from Table 1 and the sample description. The card records the denominators and the discrepancy with the paper’s narrative. [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.26777v1#page=5)

A second reviewer inspected the image of Table 1 and confirmed the means, measures of dispersion, counts of fully solved tasks and Mini-SWE-Agent’s seven-task lead over CodeSpec on Fast. [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.26777v1#page=5)
