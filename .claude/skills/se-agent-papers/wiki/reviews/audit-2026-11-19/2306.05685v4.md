# Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena

Independent audit on 19 November 2026 • 2306.05685v4 · [Paper record](../../sources/arxiv-2306.05685.md)

§§1–2 and Table 1. MT-Bench contains 80 two-turn questions in eight categories. Chatbot Arena collects anonymous pairwise comparisons using questions submitted by users. §3 and Tables 2–4 examine the effect of answer order and verbosity, and how judges assess reasoning. Providing a reference answer and reversing answer order can reduce some errors. Section 4 and Tables 5–7 distinguish pairwise from single-answer evaluation and specify how ties are handled. On the first MT-Bench turn, GPT-4’s pairwise agreement with humans is 85% across 859 comparisons after ties are excluded, and 66% across 1,343 when ties and inconsistencies are included. The corresponding Arena results are 87% across 1,944 and 64% across 3,066. The 23-point Arena difference compares two populations of judgments. Sections 5–7 and Table 8 relate preferences to MMLU and TruthfulQA and discuss the effects of using a judge in model development. Appendix A gives the instructions used by the judges. B illustrates individual cases of inconsistency, error and preference. C documents recruitment, consent and the interface. Appendix D and Tables 9–13 cover instruction variants, examples and agreement with experts after excluding the authors. Appendix F, Table 15 and additional results examine the 13B model as a judge before fine-tuning and a classifier trained on 20,000 votes.
