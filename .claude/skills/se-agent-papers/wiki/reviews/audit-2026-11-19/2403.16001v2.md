# Fine-Grained Assertion-Based Test Selection

Audit opened on 19 November 2026 for version `2403.16001v2`, 12 pages. [Paper record](../../sources/arxiv-2403.16001.md) · [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16001v2) · [Authors, license and checksum](../../raw/metadata/2403.16001.metadata.json)

## Evidence underlying the conclusions

- **I–II. Problem and example.** After a method changes in Apache Commons Math, coarse-grained selection picks an entire class of 138 tests. Seven tests depend on the change, and in one of them only some assertions are affected. Figures 1–2 distinguish a complete test method from a slice containing one assertion. [PDF, p. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.16001v2#page=1) [PDF, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.16001v2#page=2)
- **III. Algorithms and implementation.** Algorithm 1 identifies changed classes and methods from source code. SELERTION combines static statement slices with dependencies observed during execution (Figure 3). Depending on the test, Algorithm 2 selects a slice, a whole method or a class. Inheritance, parameterization and cross-test references require more conservative selection. The implementation uses JDT, Maven and JUnit 4. [PDF, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.16001v2#page=2) [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.16001v2#page=3) [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.16001v2#page=4) [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.16001v2#page=5)
- **IV, RQ1–2. Sample and precision.** The study used passing tests from 11 projects whose full suites each took at least 30 seconds to run. The authors also introduced synthetic changes in three systems. Table I lists 8,328 tests, of which 3,172 could be split. The pooled fraction is 3,172/8,328 = 38.09%. The reported 48% is the average of the project-level percentages. In Table II and Figure 4, SELERTION selects an average of 15.80% of tests and 15.10% of assertions. HyRTS failed on three projects. [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.16001v2#page=5) [PDF, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.16001v2#page=6) [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.16001v2#page=7)
- **IV, RQ3–5. Time and effectiveness.** Table III measures analysis and execution time (AE). Table IV shows that the selected tests killed the same mutants as the full suite in the cases evaluated. Table V distinguishes dependency collection (146.18 seconds on average) from initial overhead (918.96 seconds). The assertion-level variant saves about one second on average compared with the method-level variant. - **V–VII. Discussion and concluding sections.** The real-fault check used one shared Defects4J project. A change to the Java version alone can leave the source unchanged and go undetected by this method. Fine-grained selection is unsupported for multithreaded tests. The authors compare the method with other levels of regression test selection. Reference 22 links to the implementation. The PDF has no separate appendix. ## Independent verification

Recalculation from Table III gives 1 − 74.33/200.36 = 62.90%, 1 − 74.33/119.74 = 37.92% and 1 − 77.51/83.51 = 7.18%. The first two comparisons cover 11 projects and the last covers eight. The mean of the project-level AE/RetestAll percentages is 41.49%, corresponding to a reduction of 58.51%. The paper card distinguishes this calculation from the ratio of mean times.
