# Automated Unit Test Improvement using Large Language Models at Meta

Audit opened on 19 November 2026 · version `2402.09171v1`. [Paper record](../../sources/arxiv-2402.09171.md) · [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09171v1) · [Retrieval metadata](../../raw/metadata/2402.09171.metadata.json)

## Findings and evidence

- **1–2.1.5. Objective and system.** Two internal Meta models extend Kotlin test classes. Figure 1 distinguishes generation, compilation, passing all five runs and increased line coverage relative to the existing build target. The resulting suite retains earlier tests, and a human decides whether to accept each proposal. Passing five runs leaves longer-run stability uncertain. [PDF, p. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.09171v1#page=1) [PDF, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.09171v1#page=2) [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.09171v1#page=3)
- **3.1–3.2. Sample and initial deployment.** After the first eight proposals, the authors moved the checks from class level to individual tests. In November, 16 of 17 proposals were accepted. One added coverage without an assertion. Table 1 reports 1,460 additional lines covered, of which a single test accounted for 1,326. [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.09171v1#page=4) [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.09171v1#page=5)
- **3.3. Experiment.** The study used 86 Reels and Stories components, two model configurations and four prompting strategies. The text defines the measure as the percentage of classes with at least one test passing each filter. The three rates were 75%, 57% and 25%. The Figure 2 caption and abstract use a different unit, “test cases.” Table 2 describes the prompts, including one that contains only the test class. [PDF, p. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.09171v1#page=1) [PDF, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.09171v1#page=6) [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.09171v1#page=7)
- **3.4–4. December deployment and counts.** Instagram accepted 36 of 42 proposals and Facebook 144 of 280, using all generated proposals as the denominator. The unit in Tables 3–6 is one test-generation attempt. Unequal numbers of attempts across default settings and the gradual depletion of uncovered lines complicate causal comparisons. [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.09171v1#page=7) [PDF, p. 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.09171v1#page=8)
- **5–6. Observations and related work.** The authors observed identical test bodies, coverage of a different component, tests without assertions and partial method coverage. They consider additional filters and note that earlier studies generating whole test suites depend on the benchmark and codebase size. [PDF, p. 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.09171v1#page=8) [PDF, p. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.09171v1#page=9) [PDF, p. 10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.09171v1#page=10)
- **7–8. Open problems and conclusions.** Line coverage is a proxy for quality. Alternatives include mutation analysis, task-specific generation, and filters that prevent duplication of outdated coding styles. The paper ends with references and has no appendices. ## Verification of counts

For the three deployments, Section 4 reports that 196 of 1,979 classes improved, or **9,90%**. The abstract of the same version reports 11.5% of classes and provides insufficient data to reproduce that percentage. The reported 73% measures recommendation acceptance. On Facebook, the acceptance rate is 51.43% across all 280 proposals and 69.23% when restricted to the 144 accepted and 64 rejected proposals. [PDF, p. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.09171v1#page=1) [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.09171v1#page=7)

Summing Table 3 across platforms independently gives 1,321 successful attempts out of 32,531. The totals in Table 5 (models) and Table 6 (model by platform) agree at 1,320 out of 31,827. The paper provides no attempt-level data to reconcile the discrepancy. The card therefore identifies the unit and sample for each reported result. The header of arXiv v1 contains a placeholder DOI.
