# Can LLM Agents Really Debate? A Controlled Study of Multi-Agent Debate in Logical Reasoning

Audit opened on 19 November 2026 · version `2511.07784v1`. [Paper record](../../sources/arxiv-2511.07784.md) · [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07784v1) · [Retrieval metadata](../../raw/metadata/2511.07784.metadata.json)

## Review of sections and appendices

- Sections 1–3. The study measures debate quality through verifiable roles in a puzzle. The dataset includes 300 arrangements for each group size from four to nine players, or 1,800 in total. The related work distinguishes final-answer accuracy from the course of the debate. [PDF, p. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07784v1#page=1) [PDF, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07784v1#page=2) [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07784v1#page=3)
- Sections 4–5, Figures 1–2 and Tables 1–3. Agents independently assign roles, discuss each player, revise their assignments and vote. A supervisor intervenes if no majority emerges. The study varies group composition and size, access to stated confidence, ordering, depth and difficulty. Each configuration uses 100 puzzles. The tables distinguish model configurations and the behavioral dimensions evaluated. [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07784v1#page=4) [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07784v1#page=5) [PDF, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07784v1#page=6)
- Section 6, Figures 3–6 and Tables 4–5. In the regression for final role correctness (745 observations), the coefficient for initial correctness is 0.600 (SE 0.100), and that for the number of agents is 0.066 (SE 0.014), with R² = 0.393. The model for stability of agreement has R² = 0.290. The initial-chaos coefficient has opposite signs across the models (+0.085 and −0.117). Neither ordering nor confidence disclosure reaches the stated significance threshold. Transitions between correct and incorrect majorities and DeepSeek’s ratings of utterances describe the process. They provide no independent experiment on judge quality. [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07784v1#page=7) [PDF, p. 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07784v1#page=8) [PDF, p. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07784v1#page=9) [PDF, p. 10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07784v1#page=10)
- Sections 7–8. The evidence concerns a staged task with a unique solution, selected models, and specific team compositions. In the team-size experiment the researchers added the strongest agent, so the observed effect can reflect both size and composition. References and limitations follow the discussion. - Appendices A–D disclose the use of LLMs and formally define strict, partial, and temporal agreement metrics. They specify initial instructions, replies, self-correction, voting, and supervisor mode. The argument-quality rubric uses the correct roles and applies different scales to agreement and disagreement.
