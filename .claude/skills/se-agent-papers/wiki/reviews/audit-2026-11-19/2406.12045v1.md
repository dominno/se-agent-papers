## Sections 1–4. Task structure

In τ-bench the user simulator can vary its utterances while the goals, initial database and task rules remain fixed. Success requires the interaction to produce the target state and, where specified, communicate required information. Pass^k measures success on all k trials and pass@k at least one success. Both are averaged across tasks. [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.12045v1#page=3) [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.12045v1#page=4) [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.12045v1#page=5)

Table 1 lists 115 retail and 50 airline tasks. Retail and airline have seven and six write tools, respectively, and eight and seven read tools. The authors designed the schemas, policies and interfaces before generating the data and manually preparing and checking the tasks. ## Sections 5–6. Results and limits of metrics

Table 2 gives GPT-4o with function calling pass^1 of 61.2% in retail and 35.2% in airline tasks. The authors’ mean is (61.2 + 35.2)/2 = 48.2%. Equal domain weighting matters because task counts differ. Figure 4 concerns retail and gives pass^8 below 25%. Table 3 measures the effect of removing policy from the prompt. Figure 5 manually classifies 36 failed trajectories of one agent. These use different populations from the overall model comparison. [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.12045v1#page=7) [PDF, p. 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.12045v1#page=8)

The authors discuss dependence on the simulator and limitations in reproducing intentions and rules. Figure 6 relates difficulty to the number of required records without isolating a cause. ## Appendices A–D

Appendix A (Figure 7) plots success rates for individual retail tasks. The GPT-4 Turbo results in this plot use at least 40 repetitions per task. Appendix B documents schemas, APIs, policies and the data generator. Appendix C gives test records and three conversations illustrating different errors: an exchange decision, a tool argument and partial completion of a complex request. Appendix D gives flight records, customer constraints and a successful trajectory through final payment confirmation. The data are simulated. ## Independent source check

The reward is the product of two scores, one for the database state and one for communicating the required information to the simulated user. Table 2 gives 61.2% for retail and 35.2% for airline. Their unweighted mean is (61.2 + 35.2)/2 = 48.2%, with 115 and 50 tasks, respectively, in Table 1. The Figure 4 caption confirms that retail performance falls below 25% when eight successes on the same task are required. The discussion identifies potential reasoning and memory errors in the model simulating the user.
