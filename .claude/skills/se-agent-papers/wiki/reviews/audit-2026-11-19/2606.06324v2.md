# From Failed Trajectories to Reliable LLM Agents: Diagnosing and Repairing Harness Flaws

Audit opened on 19 November 2026 · version `2606.06324v2`. [Paper record](../../sources/arxiv-2606.06324.md) · [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06324v2) · [Retrieval metadata](../../raw/metadata/2606.06324.metadata.json)

## Findings and evidence

- **I–II. What the system repairs and why.** Table I sets out the responsibilities of seven layers in the agent's execution environment. Of approximately 57,780 history entries from 30 public projects, 26,174 were classified as relating to that environment. Figure 1 and Table II relate the distribution of fault types to families of allowable changes. Selecting popular projects limits representativeness. - **III.A–B. Diagnosis.** Figures 2–3 show how traces lead to narrowly scoped changes and illustrate a case that ended before the required state change occurred. HTIR distinguishes data flow from control flow, maps steps to implementation locations and combines repeated diagnoses into a single fault description. - **III.C–D. Repair.** The specification defines permitted and prohibited artifacts, required behavior and tests. Candidates undergo scope and implementation checks and are evaluated on a validation set. Memory also retains rejected proposals and observed regressions. [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.06324v2#page=7) [PDF, p. 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.06324v2#page=8)
- **IV. Experiment.** The held-out test sets contained 60 GAIA, 100 SWE-bench Verified, 90 AppWorld, and 34 Terminal-Bench tasks. The reported execution scores are means over three runs. For the diagnostic analysis, three annotators assessed 20 failed trajectories per benchmark. [PDF, p. 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.06324v2#page=8) [Inter-annotator agreement](https://github.com/HarnessFix/HarnessFix/blob/9167a0b9a58748c73b56c3ee04fdc3437ba0c56e/docs/diagnosis_annotation.md) [Model Settings](https://github.com/HarnessFix/HarnessFix/blob/9167a0b9a58748c73b56c3ee04fdc3437ba0c56e/exp/model_configuration.md)
- **V.A–D. Measurements.** Table III compares each model with its own starting point. Table IV compares the baseline environments and the cost of offline adaptation. Tables V and VI compare diagnostic representations and ablations, respectively. On GAIA, Table VII applies the change selected for GPT-5 mini to four other models. Their gains of 5.5–9.5 percentage points measure transfer of the same change across models, without per-model tuning. - **VI–VIII. Discussion and limitations.** Table VIII identifies which layers changed for each benchmark. The comparison includes environments designed by humans and methods that modify prompts and code. Version 2 concludes with the conclusions and references on pp. 11–13. ## Independent check of the publication card and supplementary materials

Subtracting the no-regression-validation scores from the full model in Table VI gives gains of 6.1, 4.0, 3.7, and 2.0 percentage points for GAIA, SWE, AppWorld, and TB2, respectively. The accompanying file confirms these values. Across the full five-model matrix in Table III, the minimum is **5,9** percentage points (DeepSeek, AppWorld). The 6.3–18.4 range in the abstract applies to the four benchmarks with the default GPT-5 mini model.
