# FActScore: Fine-grained Atomic Evaluation of Factual Precision in Long Form Text Generation

Independent audit on 19 November 2026 • 2305.14251v2 · [Paper record](../../sources/arxiv-2305.14251.md)

Sections 1–3 and Tables 1–2. For each answer the metric measures the proportion of atomic facts supported by a specified source version, then averages across answers. The authors examined biographies and counted refusals separately. ChatGPT scored 58.3%, between InstructGPT (42.5%) and PerplexityAI (71.5%). Scores fell for lesser-known people. Among 30 PerplexityAI errors, the analysis distinguishes claims contradicted by a single sentence from those requiring a longer passage to verify. Sections 4–5 and Tables 3–6. Passage retrieval and a language model estimate the score. In Table 3, Retrieve→LM + NP has absolute errors of 1.4, 0.4 and 9.9 points for the three models. For PerplexityAI the error is 71.5 − 61.6 = 9.9. A correct ranking can still contain unequal estimation errors. Tables 4–5 cover other models and response rates. Table 6 gives a biography with high factual precision despite omitting important information. Appendix A and Tables 7–8 describe fact segmentation, annotation and disagreements. In 3 of 30 cases lacking support in Wikipedia, another source supplied evidence. Appendix B and Tables 9–13 distinguish aggregate-score error from classification error for individual facts and examine retrievers, sampling variance and cross-domain transfer. Direct evidence was absent from retrieved passages in 70% of 30 errors made by the selected evaluator. Appendix C and Table 14 evaluate revision after the correct labels are supplied. Table 15 and Figures 6–7 document fact segmentation, instructions and the annotation screen.
