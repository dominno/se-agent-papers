# Decentralized Multi-Agent Coordination and Communication in Shared Codebases

## Review of sections and appendices

- Sections 1–3 and Figures 1–3. The study compares work on pairs of conflicting or complementary requirements. In the oracle condition, one agent sees both specifications. Distributed runs vary information visibility, communication, integration timing and coordination. The cases use real repositories, but the requirements were deliberately designed to demand coordination. - Section 4, Tables 1–2. On 50 conflicting requirement pairs, Opus 4.8 in Claude Code scores 62% (31/50) in the oracle condition and 16% (8/50) when the agents work in parallel without knowing about their partner. Sharing messages raises performance to 20%, and sequential execution in A→B order yields 54%. On 35 complementary pairs, the corresponding oracle and unaware-agent scores are 86% and 57%. Six open configurations were evaluated on a broader sample. - Sections 5–6 and limitations. The result concerns two agents, deliberately constructed tasks, and the merge procedure. Success is assessed by testing the integrated artifact. The study does not estimate real-world conflict frequency or the effect of adding more agents. - Appendices A–E, Tables 3–4 and Figure 4. The 155 pairs from 11 repositories in four languages comprise 85 conflicting and 70 complementary pairs. The appendices describe the baseline benchmark releases, seeds, task generation, Ansible and Vuls examples and communication topologies.
