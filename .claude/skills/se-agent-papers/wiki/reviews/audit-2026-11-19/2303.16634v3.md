# G-Eval: NLG Evaluation using GPT-4 with Better Human Alignment

Review of version `2303.16634v3` • 19 November 2026 • 11 pages. [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16634v3) · [Metadata and license](../../raw/metadata/2303.16634.metadata.json) · [Paper record](../../sources/arxiv-2303.16634.md)

## Section review

- **1–2. Method and Figure 1.** The model first generates evaluation instructions from the task description and criterion. It then fills in a form for the text being evaluated. The probabilities of the possible ratings weight the mean score to reduce ties between texts. [PDF, p. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.16634v3#page=1) [PDF, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.16634v3#page=2) [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.16634v3#page=3)
- **3. Comparison settings.** The GPT-3.5 condition used text-davinci-003 at temperature 0. For GPT-4, the authors estimated the rating distribution from 20 samples at temperature 1 and top-p of 1. SummEval evaluates CNN/DailyMail summaries, Topical-Chat evaluates the next dialogue turn, and QAGS measures summary consistency with the source. Baselines include lexical and semantic metrics and fine-tuned judges. [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.16634v3#page=3) [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.16634v3#page=4) [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.16634v3#page=5)
- **3.4–3.6. Tables 1–3.** On SummEval, the authors report aggregate ρ = 0.514 for G-EVAL-4 and 0.474 for UniEval. On Topical-Chat, GPT-3.5 correlates more strongly on engagement and groundedness, although GPT-4 has the higher aggregate correlation. On QAGS-XSum, GPT-4 has ρ = 0.537, compared with 0.488 for UniEval and 0.406 for GPT-3.5. On QAGS-CNN, Pearson correlation is higher for BARTScore (0.735) than for G-EVAL-4 (0.631). [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.16634v3#page=4) [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.16634v3#page=5) [PDF, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.16634v3#page=6) [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.16634v3#page=7)
- **4. Ablations and bias.** Removing the evaluation steps lowers scores on every SummEval dimension in Table 1. Dropping probability weighting increases ties and changes the interpretation of tau. In Figure 2, GPT-3.5 summaries receive a higher mean rating in each of the three groups defined by human preference. Low inter-rater agreement (Krippendorff’s α of 0.07 in the cited dataset) limits conclusions about the cause. - **5–6, Bibliography and Appendix A.** Related work covers lexical, embedding-based, task-specific and language-model-based metrics. The authors propose further study of agreement between model judges and humans. The appendix gives complete examples for summary coherence (1–5), dialogue engagement (1–3) and detection of factual contradictions with the source text. ## Independent verification

Table 1 gives four ρ values for full G-EVAL-4, namely 0.582, 0.507, 0.455 and 0.547. Their arithmetic mean is **0,52275**, while the printed AVG column and abstract state **0,514**. The paper gives too little detail about aggregation to reproduce 0.514 from the four cells alone. The paper card records both the reported value and the discrepancy. The caption of Table 2 names Spearman and Kendall, although the columns are labeled r and ρ. The comparisons across dimensions follow the column labels.
