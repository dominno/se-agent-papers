# Debugging the Debuggers: Failure-Anchored Structured Recovery for Software Engineering Agents

Audit opened on 19 November 2026 · version `2605.08717v2`. [Paper record](../../sources/arxiv-2605.08717.md) · [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08717v2) · [Retrieval metadata](../../raw/metadata/2605.08717.metadata.json)

## Findings and evidence

- **1–2.3. Problem and source material.** Figure 1 distinguishes observation, diagnosis, and intervention after a failure. Two engineers independently labeled the primary cause without seeing the PROBE diagnosis, then reconciled disagreements. The six categories in Table 1 sum to 257. The most frequent were omitted validation (83), tool error handling (47), and incorrect process state (42). [PDF, p. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08717v2#page=1) [PDF, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08717v2#page=2) [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08717v2#page=3) [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08717v2#page=4)
- **3.1–3.4. Design.** Figures 2–3 show how metrics, logs, traces, intent, tool state, and an optional final score yield a structured diagnosis. The rules check the evidentiary basis, feasibility, scope, and termination signal. The model’s confidence score is not a calibrated probability. [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08717v2#page=4) [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08717v2#page=5) [PDF, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08717v2#page=6)
- **4.1–4.2. Experimental conditions and results.** After an initial failure, the agent was allowed one repair attempt with the same budget and evaluator. The sample contains 102 SWE-bench, 106 EnterpriseOps-Gym and 49 AIOpsLab tasks. Table 2 compares outcome feedback, AgentOps and LangSmith summaries, and PROBE. On AIOpsLab, LangSmith and PROBE have the same Top-1 score (44.90%) but different repair rates (16.33% and 30.61%). [PDF, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08717v2#page=6) [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08717v2#page=7)
- **4.3–4.4. Distribution and cost.** Figure 4 gives one case that can be checked. Table 3 breaks down correct diagnoses and repairs by cause. The median time to generate feedback was 210.43 seconds. Across the three environments, prompts grew by an average of 1.07 thousand, 0.45 thousand and 0.39 thousand tokens, respectively. [PDF, p. 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08717v2#page=8) [PDF, p. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08717v2#page=9)
- **5.1–5.3. IcM prototype.** Table 4 describes four interfaces, from starting a run to returning its report. The authors demonstrate that the prototype integrates with the diagnostic workflow. They do not measure time to resolve real production incidents. [PDF, p. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08717v2#page=9) [PDF, p. 10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08717v2#page=10)
- **6–8. Scope, conclusions, and availability.** The benefit of multiple rounds and the effect of changing the diagnostic model remain open questions. Section 8 identifies the public code and data. The bibliography follows, with no appendix. ## Independent verification of the paper record

Table 3 yields a correct cause category in 168/257 cases (65.37%) and a successful repair in 56/257 (21.79%). Both summary-based baselines in Table 2 achieved 24/257 repairs. The difference is 32/257, or 12.45 percentage points. These results are conditional on a failed first attempt. [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08717v2#page=7) [PDF, p. 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08717v2#page=8)

In the “Patch/submission workflow” row, the authors counted 15 repairs and only 12 diagnoses matching the annotation. At least three repairs therefore succeeded without matching the agreed top-ranked cause category. This qualifies the conclusion about the role of correct diagnosis.
