# DurableFS: A File System for Persistent Memory

Audit opened on 19 November 2026 · version `1811.00757v1`. [Paper record](../../sources/arxiv-1811.00757.md) · [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00757v1) · [Retrieval metadata](../../raw/metadata/1811.00757.metadata.json)

## Findings and evidence
- **1. Introduction.** A successful close makes the operations since the file was opened durable and atomic. The authors identify a joint close for multiple files as future work. [PDF, p. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.00757v1#page=1)
- **2. Related work.** The authors compare the assumptions of BPFS, PMFS, HiKV, DAX, NOVA, and SOUPfs. Differences include when data and metadata become durable, which matters when choosing a comparable NOVA configuration. [PDF, p. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.00757v1#page=1) [PDF, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.00757v1#page=2)
- **3 and 3.1–3.2.** The module runs under the Linux VFS and uses 4 KiB blocks. Figure 1 shows the memory layout. Table I gives the fields of a log record, including the operation, transaction and pointer to the preceding entry. Figure 2 traces a single write through block allocation, writing the data, and updating the maps and inode. [PDF, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.00757v1#page=2) [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.00757v1#page=3) [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.00757v1#page=4)
- **3.3–3.5.** The application is responsible for its own transactional consistency and isolation. After writing the commit record, the system applies metadata changes. On restart, it replays entries for committed transactions that lack a completion record. The argument for surviving another restart during recovery relies on these changes being idempotent. [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.00757v1#page=4) [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.00757v1#page=5)
- **4. Experiments.** The experiment used a Xeon with Linux 4.13.0 and allocated 4 of 32 GB of DRAM to emulate persistent memory. Table II specifies 10 threads and read/write ratios of 1:1, 1:2 and 10:1, among other parameters. Figure 3 reports means over ten runs for three systems or variants and three workloads. No measure of dispersion is given. [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.00757v1#page=5)
- **5. Conclusions and bibliography.** The authors attribute the overhead to cache-line flushes. They did not test the proposed distributed key–value store. The final page contains only references, including NOVA, ARIES, and testing tools. Version 1 ends with the bibliography and has no appendix. [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.00757v1#page=5) [PDF, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.00757v1#page=6)

## Independent verification of the paper record

Figure 3 compares full DurableFS with DurableFS without data-cache flushing. Reading the legend and bars in that order gives 6.62/7.32 for Fio, 39.03/42.10 for Filebench fileserver and 12.53/12.96 for webserver, in units of 100 MB/s. The drops relative to the second variant are 9.56%, 7.29% and 3.32%, each with its own denominator. Their unweighted mean is 6.72%, consistent with the authors’ approximation of 7%. The report does not provide individual run measurements. [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.00757v1#page=5)

The publication card now specifies the baseline and hardware conditions. The recovery mechanism is justified by its design. The available plot measures throughput on DRAM and therefore cannot establish the storage medium’s behavior during an actual power failure.
