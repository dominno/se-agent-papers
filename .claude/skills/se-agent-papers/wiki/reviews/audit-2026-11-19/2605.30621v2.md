# Harness Updating Is Not Harness Benefit: Disentangling Evolution Capabilities in Self-Evolving LLM Agents

## Review of sections and appendices

- §§1–3. With model weights fixed, a model can update external instructions or use those updates when performing tasks. Figures 1–2 and Equations 6–8 define two aggregates. The benefit provided by an update author is averaged across three executors. The benefit to an executor is its largest gain across updates from three authors. - Section 4. Seven models were evaluated as authors of instruction updates and six as executors. Figures 3–5 compare means, a single-task example and variability for a fixed executor. Table 1 gives the largest gains on SWE for Qwen3-235B (+19.3 points), on MCP for GPT-OSS-120B (+7.0) and on SkillsBench for Haiku 4.5 (+15.1). Figures 6–7 and Tables 2–3 distinguish retrieving instructions from adhering to them. For Qwen3-32B on SkillsBench, 0.251 of trajectories include instruction retrieval. The adherence score falls from 0.52 at retrieval to 0.13 by the end. The extended review of prior work distinguishes weight updates from modifications to the agent’s working environment. Tables 8–14 document instructions for agents performing tasks and for authors of updates, as well as model-based evaluation of whole trajectories and individual phases. The authors also disclose their use of AI assistants. ## Independent verification

In Table 5, the Qwen3.5-9B update on SWE yields ((76.4 − 74.2) + (73.2 − 73.2) + (38.8 − 20.7))/3 = 6.77 points, matching the reported 6.8 after rounding. On the same benchmark in Table 6, the best pairing with the weaker executor (40.0%) still trails the worst pairing with the stronger executor (75.2%) by 35.2 points. The card now gives benchmark-specific results in place of the earlier claim about a single winning group of models.
