# Fact or Fiction: Verifying Scientific Claims

Review of version `2004.14974v6` • 19 November 2026 • 17 pages. [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.14974v6) · [Metadata and license](../../raw/metadata/2004.14974.metadata.json) · [Paper record](../../sources/arxiv-2004.14974.md)

## Section review

- **1–3, Figures 1–3 and Tables 1–2.** Annotators wrote claims from citing sentences without seeing the abstract used as evidence. A second phase assigned labels and minimal rationales. The choice of journals and distractors concentrates the dataset on biomedicine. SciFact contains 1,409 claims (809/300/300 for training, validation and testing). Table 1 shows that abstracts can provide evidence both for and against a single claim. - **4–5. The task and VeriSci.** TF-IDF retrieves three abstracts. Subsequent modules select sentences and predict support, refutation or insufficient information. Evaluation distinguishes abstract classification, sentence selection and rationale agreement. The oracle variant is given the evidence document, while the open variant must first retrieve it. [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.14974v6#page=5) [PDF, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.14974v6#page=6) [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.14974v6#page=7)
- **6, Tables 3–5.** On validation data with gold rationales, the classifier achieved 81.9% accuracy after training on FEVER and SciFact and 67.6% after FEVER alone. Sentence selection performed better after SciFact alone (F1 of 72.1 versus 69.7 with both datasets). On the open test, abstract-level F1 for the label plus rationale was 46.5 for VeriSci and 36.4 for the zero-shot system. With the abstract supplied, VeriSci scored 72.7. The transfer study used 36 manually written claims. A human annotator judged the proposed material credible for 23/36. The error analysis covers domain knowledge, relation direction and statistical interpretation. Each decision concerns a particular claim–abstract pair. ## Independent verification

In the public files `claims_train.jsonl`, `claims_dev.jsonl` and `claims_test.jsonl` 809, 300 and 300 records were counted, respectively, and in `corpus.jsonl` **5183** abstracts. Figure 2 lists 601 initial abstracts, 140 co-cited abstracts and 4,259 distractors, which sum to **5000**. Section 3 and the data file both give 5,183. For the main test result, (46.5 − 36.4)/36.4 = 27.75% relative improvement, consistent with the rounded 28% in the text.
