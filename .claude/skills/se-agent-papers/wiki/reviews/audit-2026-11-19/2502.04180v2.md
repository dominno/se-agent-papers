## Section review

- Sections 1–2 and Figure 1. The authors combine search over team architectures with selection of operators for a specific question. Related work covers agent systems, automation of their design, and links with AutoML. [PDF, p. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.04180v2#page=1) [PDF, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.04180v2#page=2) [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.04180v2#page=3)
- §3, Figures 2–3 and Algorithm 1. A controller samples operators one layer at a time and can stop early. The objective combines quality and token cost. Monte Carlo estimates guide updates to the sampling distribution. Textual feedback changes the prompts, temperatures and operator structure. [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.04180v2#page=3) [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.04180v2#page=4) [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.04180v2#page=5)
- Sections 4.1–4.2 and Tables 1–2. The main configuration uses four layers and four samples. The unweighted MaAS mean across the five math and coding tests is (92.30 + 51.82 + 98.80 + 92.85 + 82.17)/5 = 83.588. On GAIA, the reported mean is 20.69%. In the hardest category MaAS scored 6.25% versus 10.20% for TapeAgent. The Table 1 caption names GPT-4o-mini as a common base model. Appendix C.2 also lists Qwen and Llama as components of LLM-Blender. [PDF, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.04180v2#page=6) [PDF, p. 17](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.04180v2#page=17)
- Section 4.3, Figure 4, and Table 3. On MATH, accuracy increased from 51.28% for AFlow to 51.82%. The MaAS training API cost was 3.38/22.50 = 15.0% of AFlow’s, and its inference cost was 0.42/1.66 = 25.3%. Optimization took 53 and 184 minutes, respectively. Adding the workloads in both columns gives USD 3.80 for MaAS and USD 24.16 for AFlow. These ratios depend on pricing, platform, and query volume. [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.04180v2#page=7)
- Sections 4.4–5, Figures 5–7 and Table 4. Examples differ in depth and tool use. The sensitivity analysis identifies four layers as a compromise. On HumanEval, removing the cost penalty raises pass@1 from 92.85% to 92.94% and increases the cost per instance from 1.01 to 1.38 thousandths of a dollar. Disabling the text gradient lowers the score. Section 5 and the impact statement discuss efficiency and applications. References precede Appendix A. The appendices describe the operator space, including early exit, the construction of representations, and instructions for text-based modifications.
