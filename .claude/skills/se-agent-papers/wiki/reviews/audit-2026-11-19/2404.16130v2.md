# From Local to Global: A Graph RAG Approach to Query-Focused Summarization

Audit opened on 19 November 2026 for version `2404.16130v2`, 26 pages. [Paper record](../../sources/arxiv-2404.16130.md) · [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16130v2) · [Authors, license and checksum](../../raw/metadata/2404.16130.metadata.json)

## Study design

- Sections 1–3. A global question requires synthesis across the corpus. The method splits source documents into chunks, extracts entities and relations, and aggregates their descriptions within hierarchically detected communities. It ranks partial answers derived from community summaries and uses them to produce the final answer. - Sections 4–5 and Tables 1–4. The authors generated 125 questions each for news and podcast transcripts. In the first experiment a judge compares pairs on criteria including completeness and diversity, with directness as a control. The second counts claims and groups them by similarity. For news, C0 averages 34.18 claims per answer versus 25.23 for vector retrieval. On podcasts the means are 32.21 and 26.50. Counting distinct claims does not establish their truth. - Sections 6–7. The authors identify the limited scope of their two corpora and the need to check inaccurate content. The findings apply to global questions. Appendices A–D describe relation extraction, community detection, window-size selection and a comparison of two answers with the judge’s rationale (Table 5). ## Independent check

In Table 2, the query-context cost at C0 is 26,657/1,014,611 = **2,63%** of the TS baseline for podcasts and 39,770/1,707,694 = **2,33%** for news. The respective savings are 97.37% and 97.67%. For C3, the same calculations yield savings of only 26.5% and 33.2%. These ratios exclude the cost of building the graph.
