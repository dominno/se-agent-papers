## Section review

- **1–2. Objective and background.** The authors compare direct answers, sequential reasoning and voting over complete attempts with exploration of multiple intermediate states (Figure 1). [PDF, p. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.10601v2#page=1) [PDF, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.10601v2#page=2) [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.10601v2#page=3)
- **3. The method.** Each task requires a choice of reasoning step, proposal generator, state evaluation and search strategy. States can be evaluated individually or compared by voting. Algorithms 1–2 respectively limit the breadth of the search and prune branches during depth-first search. [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.10601v2#page=3) [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.10601v2#page=4)
- **4.1. Game of 24.** On 100 problems indexed 901–1000, the goal was to make 24 using exactly the four given numbers. With search breadth 1, the success rate was 45%. At breadth 5, it was 74%. CoT succeeded on 4.0% of individual attempts and produced at least one correct answer on 49% of problems when allowed 100 attempts. Table 1 defines the steps for each task. Table 2 and Figures 2–3 show the configuration, results and first-error analysis. [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.10601v2#page=5) [PDF, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.10601v2#page=6)
- **4.2–4.3. Writing and crosswords.** Human raters preferred ToT in 41 of 100 story pairs and CoT in 21. They rated the remaining 38 similarly (Figures 4–5). On 20 crosswords, ToT solved 4 complete grids. Selecting the best state with access to the solution would have yielded 7. Table 3 distinguishes letter, word and grid accuracy. Figure 6 illustrates backtracking. [PDF, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.10601v2#page=6) [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.10601v2#page=7) [PDF, p. 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.10601v2#page=8)
- **5–6. Comparative background and limitations.** The authors discuss planning, self-critique, and classical search heuristics. ToT requires additional model calls and a state representation tailored to the task. Gains may be small on easier problems. A separate discussion of broader impacts precedes the references. [PDF, p. 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.10601v2#page=8) [PDF, p. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.10601v2#page=9) [PDF, p. 10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.10601v2#page=10) [PDF, p. 11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.10601v2#page=11) [PDF, p. 12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.10601v2#page=12)
- **Appendices A–B.** Appendix A identifies the code, prompts and trajectories. Table 4 reports 90 versus 86 on GSM8K and 83 versus 82 on StrategyQA for ToT and CoT, respectively. Tables 5–6 show that results vary by model and task. With GPT-3.5 on Game of 24, Table 5 gives ToT 19%, IO 6% and CoT 3%, although Section B.2 ranks CoT ahead of IO. Table 7 gives costs for Game of 24. Table 8 gives creative-writing costs despite its Game of 24 caption. ## Independent verification of the findings

The versioned archive contains 69/100 tasks with at least one correct proposal among five ToT leaves. The authors identify the file as a later replication. The original experiment reports 74/100. For baseline CoT, the log has 100 answers for each of 100 tasks. Of 10,000 attempts, 403 were correct (4.03%), with at least one success on 49/100 tasks. The latter two figures reproduce Table 2.
