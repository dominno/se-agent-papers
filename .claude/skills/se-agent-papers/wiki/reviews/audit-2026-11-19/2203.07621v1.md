# Practical Detectability for Persistent Lock-Free Data Structures

Audit opened on 19 November 2026 · version `2203.07621v1`. [Paper record](../../sources/arxiv-2203.07621.md) · [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07621v1) · [Retrieval metadata](../../raw/metadata/2203.07621.metadata.json)

## Findings and evidence

- **1. Problem.** Durable linearizability concerns the state of the data structure. Detecting the outcome of an interrupted operation also allows its enclosing operation to resume with exactly-once effects. Figures 1–2 illustrate the distinction and the records kept by each thread. [PDF, p. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.07621v1#page=1) [PDF, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.07621v1#page=2) [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.07621v1#page=3)
- **2.1–2.5. Composing operations.** A memento stores an operation’s progress and result. Requirements include separate objects for suboperations, stable control values and recovery of iteration state. After restart, the monitor calibrates timestamps. The design assumes a single Optane module and statically known root objects. [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.07621v1#page=3) [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.07621v1#page=4) [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.07621v1#page=5)
- **3–4. Primitive operations.** Algorithms 1–2 and Figure 3 cover normal CAS, recovery and helping a thread complete its operation. Memory use per CAS location is constant, with global overhead proportional to the thread count. Algorithms 3–4 and Figure 4 specialize insert and delete operations to reduce contention on a single location, at the cost of additional structural assumptions. [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.07621v1#page=5) [PDF, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.07621v1#page=6) [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.07621v1#page=7) [PDF, p. 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.07621v1#page=8) [PDF, p. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.07621v1#page=9)
- **5–6. Optimization and memory reclamation.** Recoverable data kept in DRAM must maintain the required relationship with persistent memory both during an operation and after restart. Section 6 states requirements for persistence timing, safe epoch termination and removal of stale references from mementos. [PDF, p. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.07621v1#page=9) [PDF, p. 10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.07621v1#page=10) [PDF, p. 11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.07621v1#page=11)
- **7.1–7.2. Measurements.** The experiments used Rust 1.59, one NUMA domain, an Intel Xeon Gold 6248R and Optane DCPMM. Figure 5 covers four queue workloads, 1–64 threads and five 10-second repetitions. The initial queue state depended on the workload, as documented in the data check below. Figures 6–9 cover latency tails, single- and multithreaded throughput, and different key distributions. For Clevel and SOFT, the cost of persisting results fell primarily on operations that modify data. [PDF, p. 11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.07621v1#page=11) [PDF, p. 12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.07621v1#page=12)
- **8–9. Prior work and open questions.** The authors compare earlier detectable constructions, durable structures without detectability, and transformations from DRAM-based structures. They identify larger storage engines, new primitives and formal verification as future work. The references conclude the paper on pp. 13–15. [PDF, p. 13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.07621v1#page=13) [PDF, p. 14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.07621v1#page=14) [PDF, p. 15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.07621v1#page=15)

## Checks of the source card and supplement

The experimental results identify an exception for LogQ at 80% insertions and low thread counts. The authors classify PBcombQ as a combining queue, so its position in the plots needs separate interpretation. It differs from a standard Michael–Scott queue, and the compared implementations also differ in memory reclamation. [PDF, p. 11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.07621v1#page=11) [PDF, p. 12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.07621v1#page=12)

### Verification of the original supplement, 20 November 2026

The reviewer recovered the authors’ March 2022 page, all 15 SVG figures (including legends), and the linked file `evaluation_results.zip`. The archive contains 193,634 bytes and its SHA-256 digest is `e9cdf754d14da76c894e01d44da2620eb3f6bfecf169eb514a325e31f808329b`. The full archive passed CRC verification. It contains 10 queue CSV files, 112 hash-table throughput logs, 32 latency logs and 8 load-factor logs. These data come from the original publication. A separate PLDI 2023 artifact contains later Clevel measurements. - **Figures 8–9.** In the v1 LaTeX source, Figure 8 uses the file `hash-throughput-multi-self-similar-0.2.svg`, and Figure 9 uses `hash-throughput-multi-uniform.svg`. The supplement gives the same mapping. The 56 throughput logs for each distribution identify it, respectively, as `SELFSIMILAR(0.200000)` and `UNIFORM`. The figure captions are correct. The last paragraph on page 12 reverses their order. - **The initial state of the queues.** In column `init nodes` in all ten CSV files, the value is 0 for alternating enqueue–dequeue pairs and 10,000,000 for workloads with 20%, 50% and 80% insertions. This qualifies the statement on p. 11 about 10 million initial elements. At four threads and 80% insertions, means are 1.241 million operations/s for LogQ and 1.077 million for MSQ-mmt-vol, confirming the exception to the latter’s throughput advantage. Clevel and Clevel-mmt agree exactly at 157 of 200 points and both finish at 43.0%. At intermediate points, they differ by up to 47 percentage points despite their similar overall trends.
