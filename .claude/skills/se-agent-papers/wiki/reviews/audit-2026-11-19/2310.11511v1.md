# Self-RAG: Learning to Retrieve, Generate, and Critique through Self-Reflection

Audit opened on 19 November 2026 · version `2310.11511v1`. [Paper record](../../sources/arxiv-2310.11511.md) · [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11511v1) · [Retrieval metadata](../../raw/metadata/2310.11511.metadata.json)

## Review of sections and appendices

- §§1–3, Figures 1–2, Table 1 and Algorithm 1. The model learns when to retrieve passages and how to judge their relevance, their support for a generated segment and the usefulness of the overall answer. A critic trained on GPT-4 labels prepares the generator's training data. During generation, the model produces its own reflection tokens and uses weighted scores to select segments. - Sections 4–5, Table 2. The six tasks include QA, PubHealth, ARC, biographies and ASQA. Self-RAG 13B outperforms Ret-ChatGPT on PopQA (55.8 vs. 50.8) and PubHealth (74.5 vs. 54.7), but scores lower on ARC (73.1 vs. 75.3). On ASQA it has higher citation precision (70.3 vs. 65.1) and lower citation recall (71.3 vs. 76.6). - Figures 3–4 and §6. In the 7B/50k ablation, disabling retrieval at inference lowers the PopQA score from 45.5 to 24.7. PubHealth is less sensitive to the retrieval threshold. The weight assigned to evidence support affects the trade-off between citation and fluency. The authors discuss the quality of the critic, human evaluation and the risk of generating incorrect content. These examples demonstrate the critic's work and do not add test observations.
