## Section review

- **1–2. Problem and study sample.** Given the problem statement and public tests, the generated program must pass the hidden CodeContests tests. The authors do not fine-tune the models on the training set. The validation set contains 107 tasks and the test set 165. Figure 1 shows the stages and potential gaps in public-test coverage. Figure 2 illustrates reflection on a grid problem. - **3. Method overview.** The agent analyzes the specification and examples, proposes two to three solutions and six to eight additional tests, selects an initial implementation and iteratively revises it. An incorrect model-generated test can lead to an incorrect edit. - **4. Design decisions.** The authors describe using YAML, point-by-point analysis, modularity and rechecking generated data. A patch is accepted only if previously passing tests still pass. Figure 3 shows the response format. The paper does not measure the separate contribution of each design choice. ## Paper card verification and internal discrepancy

For GPT-4, an independent reading of Table 1 gives a validation gain of 44 − 19 = 25 percentage points, or a ratio of about 2.32. The test gain is 29 − 12 = 17 percentage points. Both variants use pass@5. The card clarifies the earlier description of a single prompt as the comparator. [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.08500v1#page=7)

The cost analysis defines pass@10@100K as 10 submissions selected from 100,000 generated solutions. The authors assume one call per generated solution, which implies 100,000 calls, yet the same discussion gives one million. The claim of a four-order-of-magnitude advantage therefore requires clarification and is excluded from the confirmed benefits. [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.08500v1#page=7)
