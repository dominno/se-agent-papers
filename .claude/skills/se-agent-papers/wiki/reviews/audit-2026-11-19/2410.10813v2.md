# LongMemEval: Benchmarking Chat Assistants on Long-Term Interactive Memory

## Section review

- §§1–3, Table 1 and Figures 1–3. The benchmark contains 500 questions testing information extraction, integration across sessions, temporal reasoning, knowledge updates and abstention. Controlling the length of the history allows comparison between reading the session containing the evidence and processing the entire history. A separate evaluation covered commercial assistants. - Section 4, Table 2, and Figure 4. A key–value schema separates indexing, retrieval, and reading. A turn or session can serve as the value, and facts can enrich the key. The schema provides a framework for analyzing existing memory designs. [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.10813v2#page=7) [PDF, p. 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.10813v2#page=8)
- §5, Tables 3–4 and Figures 5–6. For the turn-level index on LongMemEval-M, adding facts raises Recall@5 from 0.582 to 0.644 and the fraction of correct GPT-4o answers using the top five results from 0.615 to 0.657. The benefit of a narrow time filter depends on the model that extracts the date. The 8B model makes extraction errors. How the retrieved evidence is presented also affects final answer accuracy. [PDF, p. 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.10813v2#page=8) [PDF, p. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.10813v2#page=9) [PDF, p. 10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.10813v2#page=10)
- Section 6, reproducibility, and ethics. For GPT-4o without Chain-of-Note, Figure 3b gives 0.870 with oracle sessions and 0.606 on S. The relative decline is 30.34% (26.4 percentage points). It reflects noise and the difficulty of reading long contexts. Section 6 summarizes potential improvements and release practices. [PDF, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.10813v2#page=6) [PDF, p. 10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.10813v2#page=10) [PDF, p. 11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.10813v2#page=11)
- Appendices A–B, Tables 5–7 and Figures 7–10. The appendices document how the authors constructed questions, inserted sessions, distributed the data and instructed the judge. On samples of 30 responses per category, mean scoring reliability was 0.98 for one model's responses and 0.97 for the other's. The commercial services were tested on 97 questions with shorter histories of three to six sessions. - Appendices C–E, Tables 8–11 and Figures 11–14. The authors examine an alternative formulation of the memory model, how existing systems fit it, and prompts for indexing and reading. Comparisons cover additional models and retrievers, rank fusion and incorrect time filters. For questions about updated facts, a model may answer correctly despite retrieving only some of the relevant earlier sessions.
