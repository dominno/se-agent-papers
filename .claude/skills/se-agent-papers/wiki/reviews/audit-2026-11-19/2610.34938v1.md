# How Do Coding-Agent Harnesses Self-Evolve? An Empirical Study of Retrospective Adaptation

Review of version `2610.34938v1` • 19 November 2026 • 5 pages. [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34938v1) · [Metadata and license](../../raw/metadata/2610.34938.metadata.json) · [Paper record](../../sources/arxiv-2610.34938.md)

## Section review

- **I–II. Introduction and related work.** The authors transferred one change and examined the agent on a different issue from the same repository. The outcome was repetition of a specific action, such as calling an unavailable test program. Repeating an action under this classification can still be a rational choice. [PDF, p. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34938v1#page=1) [PDF, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34938v1#page=2) [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34938v1#page=3)
- **III. Selection and coding.** The first ten issues from five repositories were sampled from the “15 min–1 h” effort category. The next 20 were selected purposively. The study used Claude Code 2.1.295, Opus 5.5 and a fixed instruction revision. Two LLM classifiers agreed on 109/125 examples for both cause and mechanism (κ = 0.67 and 0.84, respectively). The first author adjudicated labels. Implementing 24 proposals yielded 11 adaptations. The evaluation included those and one adaptation written by the authors. [PDF, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34938v1#page=2)
- **III. Transfer.** Twelve adaptations produced 13 transfer trials because one was tested on two tasks. Each variant had five fresh sessions. Three adaptations received an additional control test. To count among the seven informative comparisons, the problem had to occur in at least three baseline sessions. The authors distinguish encountering a problem, having guidance that covers it, reading the guidance and repeating the action. [PDF, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34938v1#page=2) [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34938v1#page=3)
- **IV, Table I.** Counts for the six mechanisms are 38, 24, 21, 20, 12 and 10, totaling 125. Executable checks were the largest category. Only 9 of the 38 could report failure in the task environment. Of 43 linting proposals, 18 arose from a general preference stated in the instructions, 23 from session events and two were only weakly connected to the session. [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34938v1#page=3)
- **IV, Table II.** Retries fell from 5/5 to 0/5 in one SymPy case, from 3/5 to 0/5 in one Astropy case and from 5/5 to 2/5 in another Astropy case. The remaining four cases showed no reduction. One of those SymPy rows has a denominator of 4 after an authentication error was excluded, compared with 5 at baseline. A separate Sphinx control row gives 0/5 versus 4/5. In the first SymPy case, adding the lint-check command increased median time from 88 to 156 seconds. [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34938v1#page=3) [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34938v1#page=4)
- **V–VI and data.** Purposive sampling, partial unblinding of the rater and grouping after labeling limit causal interpretation. Task success differed by at most one session in each comparison. The finding concerns one agent and the test environments used. Page 5 contains the conclusion, a replication link and ten references. The PDF has no separate appendix. [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34938v1#page=4) [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34938v1#page=5)

## Independent verification

In the authors’ file `coding/coding_final.csv` the review counted 128 rows, three of which have the mechanism `ARTIFACT` and correspond to excluded comments about script approval of changes. This leaves 125 proposals. The independent count yielded 101 event-based proposals, 19 preference-based and five weakly related to the session. After merging equivalent CSV labels in Table I, navigation tips total 7 + 17 = 24 and tools or configurations total 3 + 9 = 12. The seven comparisons in Table II were read directly from the table image and distinguished from the three control rows.
