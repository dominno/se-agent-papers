# Testing with AI Agents: An Empirical Study of Test Generation Frequency, Quality, and Coverage

Independent audit on 19 November 2026 • 2603.13724v1 · [Paper record](../../sources/arxiv-2603.13724.md)

## Section-by-section review

- **1. Introduction.** The authors study tests added to existing repositories. Project histories reveal practices actually adopted, while authorship must be inferred indirectly. [PDF, p. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.13724v1#page=1)
- **2. Data selection.** Of 2,807 AIDev repositories, 650 TypeScript projects were selected. A test framework could be identified in 400, including Vitest in 179. The authors selected ten projects based on AI involvement in adding tests. This was a purposive sample. [PDF, p. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.13724v1#page=1) [PDF, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.13724v1#page=2)
- **3. RQ1 and Table 1.** The authors examined PR commits within the AIDev activity window, excluding merge commits. They identified test additions in patches and inferred authorship from committer information and line-level analysis. The proportions in the table range from 7/366 in azure-sdk-for-js to 26/26 in total-typescript-monorepo. The association with project size is observational. [PDF, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.13724v1#page=2)
- **3. RQ2 and Figures 1–2.** Across releases of nine projects, tests attributed solely to AI were compared with the remaining tests. Median effective length was 12 versus 11 lines, with negligible effect size (δ = −0.12). The median assertion count was two versus one. Both groups had median cyclomatic complexity of one. Figure 1 breaks length and assertion count down by project. Figure 2 shows three CodeBERT/t-SNE projections for appkit, alchemy and llmgateway. They visualize similarity without testing cluster significance. [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.13724v1#page=3) [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.13724v1#page=4)
- **3. RQ3 and Table 2.** Coverage before and after a change was measured for 531 observations across three repositories. The six count cells sum to 59 AI observations and 472 human observations. In appkit, mean branch coverage changed by +0.183 and +0.008 percentage points, respectively. In helper, the changes were +0.006 and +0.213. The table gives means without confidence intervals. [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.13724v1#page=4)
- **4–5. Related work and conclusions.** The authors compare the historical data with earlier test-generation experiments and identify defect detection and maintenance cost as open questions. The reference to SWT-Bench on page 4 cites [17], which the bibliography identifies as SWE-bench. SWT-Bench appears at [28]. [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.13724v1#page=4) [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.13724v1#page=5)
- **References and online appendix.** The PDF has no appendix after the references. References [40–41] link to the complexity plot and data repository. The plot shows variation between projects, including a much wider distribution for alchemy than for helper. [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.13724v1#page=3) [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.13724v1#page=5) [Complexity plot](https://github.com/suu-y/AI-human-test-comparison/blob/9abc31fd2fa053bcf3bd68209f37ecd30a1b3460/figures/cross_project_complexity_by_project.png)

## Independent data check

The ten rows in Table 1 sum to 366/2,232 = 16.398%. In the authors’ artifact, the numerator counts CSV rows with the flag `is_ai_test_commit=True`. The same SHA can appear more than once. Grouping by (project, SHA) yields 351 AI records among 1,977 distinct commits, or 17.754%. Duplicate rows have consistent flags. For 108 of the 366 AI rows, the field `method_analysis_supported` is false and the number of detected methods is zero. The file reproduces the table at row level, but for these 108 cases it does not establish the all-line attribution described in the paper.
