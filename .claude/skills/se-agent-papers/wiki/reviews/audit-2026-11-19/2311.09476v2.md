# ARES: An Automated Evaluation Framework for Retrieval-Augmented Generation Systems

Audit opened on 19 November 2026 for version `2311.09476v2`, 17 pages. [Paper record](../../sources/arxiv-2311.09476.md) · [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09476v2) · [Authors, license and checksum](../../raw/metadata/2311.09476.metadata.json)

## Audit scope

- Sections 1–3 and Figure 1. ARES evaluates context relevance, answer faithfulness and answer relevance separately. FLAN generates training examples from target-domain material. DeBERTa judges learn to distinguish positives from easy and hard negatives. Prediction-powered inference (PPI) corrects the mean prediction using the error measured against manual labels. Systems are ranked by the midpoints of the estimated intervals. - Sections 4–5, Tables 1–2. For each of six datasets, the authors constructed nine variants with known accuracy ranging from 70% to 90% in steps of 2.5 percentage points. They assessed ranking agreement with Kendall’s τ and measured the accuracy of individual judgments. AIS separately evaluates answer faithfulness. The estimated proportions of correct answers are 0.478 on WoW and 0.835 on CNN/DM, compared with actual values of 0.458 and 0.859. - §§5.3–7. An additional test covers combinations of three retrievers and three generators, as well as Facebook RAG. Each pairwise evaluation used one retrieved passage. Applying the judge trained on NQ to CodeSearchNet yielded τ = 0.28, indicating limited transfer without adaptation. Domain-specific annotations and compute for generation and training are required. ## Independent check

From Table 5, the mean τ for context for ARES is (1.00 + 0.91 + 0.82)/3 = **0,91**, for responses (0.96 + 1.00 + 1.00)/3 = **0,987**, which means **0,99** after rounding. Section 5.3 gives 0.97 for answers. In Table 1, FEVER answer relevance has τ = 0.78 for ARES versus 0.89 for sampled annotations, an exception to the average advantage. Label savings are 1 − 300/1,350 = 77.8%.
