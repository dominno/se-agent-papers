# SquirrelFS: Using the Rust Compiler to Check File-System Crash Consistency

Audit opened on 19 November 2026 · version `2406.09649v1`. [Paper record](../../sources/arxiv-2406.09649.md) · [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09649v1) · [Retrieval metadata](../../raw/metadata/2406.09649.metadata.json)

## Content review

- Sections 1–2 and Table 1. The authors qualitatively compare testing, full verification and typestate in terms of effort and time. “Complete” in the table applies only to the ordering rules encoded in the types. Figure 1 and Listing 1 show an invalid reference to an inode before it is ready. [PDF, p. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.09649v1#page=1) [PDF, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.09649v1#page=2) [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.09649v1#page=3) [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.09649v1#page=4)
- Section 3. Three SSU dependencies govern initialization, deallocation and pointer replacement. Figure 2 details a recoverable atomic rename. Listing 2 separates operational state from persistence. Figure 3 traces the dependencies of mkdir, while Figure 4 illustrates page allocation and rebuildable indexes. Typestate enforces operation ordering. The transitions and the choice of rules require separate verification. The hardware assumptions are low latency and byte addressability. [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.09649v1#page=5) [PDF, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.09649v1#page=6) [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.09649v1#page=7) [PDF, p. 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.09649v1#page=8)
- Section 4. Developing the implementation and the Alloy model together exposed distinct classes of errors. The compiler caught missing persistence operations and incorrect ordering. The model identified, among other issues, a problem deleting a rename target. Tests still found index bugs. The authors discuss the cost of complex traces and the difficulty of comparing effort without an equivalent control implementation. [PDF, p. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.09649v1#page=9) [PDF, p. 10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.09649v1#page=10) [PDF, p. 11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.09649v1#page=11)
- Sections 5.1–5.4, Figures 5a–d. The setup uses a 128 GB persistent-memory module, with the file systems configured for metadata consistency. On Filebench, the lead over the next-best system is 8% for fileserver and 13% for varmail. Results in RocksDB depend on workload. In particular, ext4-DAX outperforms SquirrelFS on Run E range scans. The authors also evaluated LMDB and individual operation latencies. [PDF, p. 11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.09649v1#page=11) [PDF, p. 12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.09649v1#page=12)
- Sections 5.5–8, Tables 2–3. With a full device, a normal mount takes 30.50 seconds and a mount with a recovery scan takes 55.50 seconds, a difference of 25 seconds. The scan was run on a cleanly unmounted image. Compiling 7,500 lines took 10 seconds, compared with 13–38 seconds for the other modules. Alloy was bounded to two operations, ten objects and 30 steps. The system passed all 67 supported xfstests. Chipmunk found four bugs outside the enforced ordering rules. [PDF, p. 13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.09649v1#page=13) [PDF, p. 14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.09649v1#page=14)
- Sections 6–7. The authors discuss Corundum, SoupFS, ArckFS, Bento and ShardStore and conclude that the rules can be enforced without changing the language. The conclusion links to the code and model. The references extend to page 18. The PDF has no appendix.
