# Prometheus 2: An Open Source Language Model Specialized in Evaluating Other Language Models

Independent audit on 19 November 2026 • 2405.01535v2 · [Paper record](../../sources/arxiv-2405.01535.md)

## Chapters and experiments

Sections 1–2 and Figure 1 introduce the LM judge and compare weight merging with joint training. Section 3, Table 1 and Figure 2 distinguish training for justified 1–5 ratings from training for pairwise answer preferences. The two datasets share 20,000 prompts and 1,000 criteria. Five rated answers per prompt yield ten distinct pairs, or 200,000 comparison examples in total. Sections 4–5. Table 2 identifies the source of the reference ratings. Preference Bench uses commercial-model ratings. The other three pairwise benchmarks use human ratings. Tables 3–4 report Pearson correlation and selection accuracy. On FLASK the 8×7B model’s correlation with humans is 0.555, versus 0.449 for Prometheus-13B and 0.679 for GPT-4. The gap to GPT-4 falls from 0.230 to 0.124, about 46%. The proportion of ties affects the comparisons on MT Bench/Auto-J. [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.01535v2#page=5) [PDF, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.01535v2#page=6)

Section 6, Tables 5–6 and Figure 3. For Mixtral, mean accuracy across three comparisons after merging is (85.52 + 71.96 + 79.98)/3 = 79.15%, versus 75.29% after joint training. For Mistral on HHH, joint training achieves 80.09% and merging 74.66%. For 7B with GPT-4 on FLASK, Table 3 gives 0.617 but Tables 5–6 give 0.659. The card uses the other unambiguous results. Section 7 and the limitations section describe agreement with judges as a proxy for evaluation quality. ## Appendices

Appendices A–B, Tables 7–10. Five annotators assessed dataset quality on 200 examples. The consistency check yielded 99.5%, and agreement with the criterion was 98.5%. Table 9 describes linear merging for 7B and Table 10 describes DARE merging for 8×7B, although Section 3 describes DARE-Linear for both. Appendices C–D and Tables 11–12 report rank-based measures separately from the primary Pearson correlation. Appendix D distinguishes the terms of use for the data and the weights. Appendix E and Tables 13–15 assess format compliance, repeatability under stochastic decoding and transitivity of preferences. Appendices F–G and Tables 16–17 examine the influence of the reference answer and the choice of aggregation method. For the 8×7B model on FLASK, removing the reference lowers r from 0.555 to 0.411. Appendices H–J give templates for generating rationales, assigning scores and choosing between two answers.
