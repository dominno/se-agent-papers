# Sentence-BERT: Sentence Embeddings using Siamese BERT-Networks

Audit opened on 19 November 2026 for version `1908.10084v1`, 11 pages. [Paper record](../../sources/arxiv-1908.10084.md) · [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.10084v1) · [Authors, license and checksum](../../raw/metadata/1908.10084.metadata.json)

## Review of sections and tables

- **1–2. Introduction and background.** Jointly encoding two sentences requires a separate forward pass for each pair. The authors discuss InferSent, the Universal Sentence Encoder and poly-encoders among the alternatives for representing or comparing sentences. [PDF, p. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.10084v1#page=1) [PDF, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.10084v1#page=2) [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.10084v1#page=3)
- **3. Architecture.** Two copies of the encoder share weights. The default pooling method averages token vectors. The NLI classifier takes both sentence vectors and their elementwise absolute difference (Figure 1). Regression uses cosine similarity between vectors (Figure 2). A third loss compares a positive and a negative example relative to an anchor. NLI training combines SNLI and MultiNLI. [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.10084v1#page=3) [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.10084v1#page=4)
- **4.1–4.2. STS.** Table 1 reports Spearman rank correlations on seven tasks without STS-specific fine-tuning. SBERT-NLI-base averages 74.89 on a 100-point scale, versus 54.81 for mean-pooled BERT vectors. Table 2 compares training on target data alone with prior NLI training. For BERT models, it reports means and dispersion over ten initializations. [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.10084v1#page=4) [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.10084v1#page=5)
- **4.3–4.4. Transfer across topics.** On AFS, the authors evaluate cross-validation and a separate protocol that trains on two topics and tests on the third. Under the latter, the base model scores 50.65 on Spearman correlation versus 57.23 for BERT (Table 3). On sentence triplets from Wikipedia sections, SBERT-large achieves 0.8078 accuracy versus 0.74 for the method of Dor et al. (Table 4). [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.10084v1#page=5) [PDF, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.10084v1#page=6)
- **5. SentEval.** A logistic classifier takes precomputed sentence vectors. In Table 5, SBERT-NLI-large averages 87.69 and InferSent 85.59. The authors distinguish this transfer setting from optimizing all model weights for a new task. [PDF, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.10084v1#page=6) [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.10084v1#page=7)
- **6. Ablations.** On STSb regression in Table 6, mean pooling scores 87.44, max pooling 69.92 and CLS pooling 86.62. Pooling choice has a smaller effect when training on NLI classification. [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.10084v1#page=7) [PDF, p. 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.10084v1#page=8)
- **7–8. Cost and conclusions.** Table 7 measures encoding of STSb sentences on an i7-5820K and a Tesla V100. Grouping sentences by length raises SBERT-base throughput from 44 to 83 sentences/s on CPU and from 1,378 to 2,042 on GPU. The training and evaluation sections precede the acknowledgments and references on pp. 9–11. Version 1 contains no separate appendix. ## Independent check of the paper card’s findings

For 10,000 sentences, there are 10,000 × 9,999 / 2 = 49,995,000 unordered pairs. Throughput in Table 7 implies about 4.90 s to encode 10,000 sentences on a V100 when grouped by length, independently confirming the order of magnitude of the reported five seconds. The additional 0.01 s for similarities and about 65 hours for BERT come from the authors’ description. Table 7 measures sentence encoding alone.
