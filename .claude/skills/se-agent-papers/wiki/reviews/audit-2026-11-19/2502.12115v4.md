## Sections and appendices

- §§1–2 and Figures 1–5. The tasks are drawn from historical paid work on one application, Expensify. To pass, an implementing developer must satisfy independently prepared end-to-end tests. A manager must choose the proposal that was actually accepted. The dollar reward is based on the historical payout. - Section 3, Tables 1–2 and Figures 6–10. On the 502-task Diamond subset, Claude 3.5 Sonnet achieved 26.2% pass@1 on 237 execution tasks and 44.9% on 265 decision tasks. On the full set of 1,488 tasks, the respective rates were 21.1% and 47.0%. The study also examines repeated attempts, inference budget, access to an application observation tool and the hypothetical cost of handing unsolved tasks to humans. ## Independent verification

For Diamond, pooling the rounded group rates gives (237 × 26.2% + 265 × 44.9%)/502 = 36.07%, consistent with the 36.1% in Table 1. Simulated payout uses a different denominator: $208,050/$500,800 ≈ 41.54%. About $58,000 comes from execution and $150,000 from proposal selection. For the full dataset, the rounded amounts are $89,000 + $314,000 = $403,000, or 40.3% of the available pool.
