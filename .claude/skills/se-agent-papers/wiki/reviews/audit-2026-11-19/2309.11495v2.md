# Chain-of-Verification Reduces Hallucination in Large Language Models

## Section review

- **1–3.** The model drafts an answer, plans verification questions, answers them and revises the draft. The joint variant answers in a shared context. The factored variant isolates verification answers from the draft, and factor+revise also identifies inconsistencies. Figure 1 illustrates the process with a list of people. - **4.1–4.3, Table 1–4 and Figure 2.** The main comparisons use Llama 65B. On 56 Wikidata questions, the two-step method raises microprecision from 0.17 to 0.36 and reduces mean incorrect items from 2.95 to 0.68. Mean correct items also fall, from 0.59 to 0.38. On closed-book MultiSpanQA, the factored variant raises F1 from 0.39 to 0.48. On biographies, factor+revise raises FACTSCORE from 55.9 to 71.4 while mean facts per response fall from 16.6 to 12.3. On Wiki-Category, the factored variant scores 0.22 with open verification questions and 0.19 with yes/no questions. ## Independent verification

In Table 3, FACTSCORE improvement is 71.4 − 55.9 = **15.5 points**, which means **27.7% relative**, with 4.3 fewer facts on average. In Table 1, the Wiki-Category/two-step means of 0.50 correct and 0.52 incorrect items give 0.50/(0.50 + 0.52) ≈ **0,49**, while the reported precision is **0,21**. If the means covered the same questions and items as micro-precision, the results should agree. The text does not explain the discrepancy. The corresponding check for Wikidata/two-step gives 0.358 ≈ 0.36.
