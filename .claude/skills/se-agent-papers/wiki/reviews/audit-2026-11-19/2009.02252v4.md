## Sections and Appendices

- §§1–3, Figure 1 and Table 1. Eleven datasets across five task families were mapped to one English Wikipedia snapshot dated 1 August 2019. Matching titles and content links answers to their sources. Dropping examples below the matching threshold changes the composition of the test set. - §§4–6. An annotation campaign added alternative evidence sources. Alongside task metrics, the study measured source completeness using R-precision and recall@k. KILT awards answer credit only when the retrieved evidence achieves full R-precision for a valid evidence set. Table 2 gives system sizes including indexes. [PDF, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.02252v4#page=6) [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.02252v4#page=7)
- §§7–11 and Tables 3–5. The results distinguish answer quality alone, retrieval quality and answer quality conditional on evidence provenance. BLINK stands out on entity linking, while RAG outperforms some baselines on open-domain questions. Limitations include the use of statistical shortcuts and the possibility that the annotations omit valid supporting evidence. ## Independent check

FEVER illustrates how the definition of success changes the ranking. RAG assigns 86.31% of labels correctly and achieves 53.45% KILT-AC, a difference of **32.86 percentage points** BERT+DPR assigns 69.68% of labels correctly and achieves the higher KILT-AC score of 58.58%. Independent checks of Tables 3, 5 and 7 found the same values. On NQ, RAG scores 44.39% EM and 32.69% KILT-EM. The card distinguishes answer correctness from success in providing evidence.
