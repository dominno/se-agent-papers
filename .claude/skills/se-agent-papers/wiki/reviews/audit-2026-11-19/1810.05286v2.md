# Predictive Test Selection

Review of version `1810.05286v2` • 19 November 2026 • 10 pages. [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.05286v2) · [Metadata and license](../../raw/metadata/1810.05286.metadata.json) · [Paper record](../../sources/arxiv-1810.05286.md)

## Section review

- **I–II. Problem and environment.** In Facebook’s multilingual monorepo, the build dependency graph defines the candidate set. The unit is an executable test target that can contain multiple test cases. Selection operates during code review and integration. A later stabilization phase runs the full suite and can detect conflicts between changes that each passed separately. Figure 1 illustrates the dependencies. [PDF, p. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.05286v2#page=1) [PDF, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.05286v2#page=2) [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.05286v2#page=3)
- **III. Definitions.** Test recall measures the fraction of individual target failures detected. Change recall measures the fraction of faulty changes for which at least one failure was detected. Selection rate measures the fraction of targets that depend on a change and were selected for testing. Figure 2 distinguishes the impact of a change from a test failure. [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.05286v2#page=3) [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.05286v2#page=4)
- **IV. Training and deployment.** A gradient-boosted tree model scores change–target pairs using features such as failure history and graph distance. Selection takes the union of targets chosen by score threshold and by rank. The final week of a three-month dataset was held out for evaluation. Full runs for nearly a quarter of sampled changes take place off-peak. The model is checked and replaced weekly. Figure 3 shows its inputs and output. [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.05286v2#page=5) [PDF, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.05286v2#page=6)
- **V. Test flakiness.** Up to ten retries following a failure distinguish targets with mixed outcomes. Flaky targets were about four times as numerous as consistently failing targets. Figures 4–5 show how a combined metric can assign equal scores to strategies detecting very different kinds of failures. [PDF, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.05286v2#page=6) [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.05286v2#page=7)
- **VI. Curves, table and ablations.** Figures 6–7 compare the two selectors. The deployed combined selector achieved recall above 95% for failures and above 99.9% for faulty changes at a selection rate below 0.33. Relative to graph-based selection, it ran about one-third as many tests using about half as many machines. Table I removes one feature at a time. Failure history improved both relative measures (1.37 and 1.62), while target count gave 1.10 for classification and 0.20 for ranking. Figures 8–9 vary the training labels for failures. At a 15% selection rate, the model trained without filtering flaky failures detected about 90% of mixed failures and 70% of persistent failures. [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.05286v2#page=7) [PDF, p. 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.05286v2#page=8) [PDF, p. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.05286v2#page=9)
- **VII–VIII. Related work, conclusions and references.** The authors discuss static and dynamic selection, the cost of tracing execution across a multilingual repository and the absence of a model for correlations among test outcomes. The final page contains acknowledgments and 15 references. The PDF includes neither an appendix nor raw production measurements. ## Independent verification

The curves and the description of Figure 9 indicate that about 30% of genuine failures were missed, roughly three times the apparent 10%. The review checked the cost unit on page 8, and the card distinguishes it from the number of executions. The paper gives no confidence intervals for the savings. [PDF, p. 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.05286v2#page=8) [PDF, p. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.05286v2#page=9)

**Discrepancy in threshold notation.** The formula in IV-B includes every target with a score ≥ ScoreCutoff. Scores range from 0 to 1. The Figure 7 caption sets ScoreCutoff to 0, which would include every target under that formula. The text treats the figure as a ranking-only experiment. The reviewer interprets this as ranking with threshold selection disabled for reproduction.
