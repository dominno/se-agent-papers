# Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks

Audit opened on 19 November 2026 · version `2005.11401v4`. [Paper record](../../sources/arxiv-2005.11401.md) · [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401v4) · [Retrieval metadata](../../raw/metadata/2005.11401.metadata.json)

## Section review

- §§1–2 and Figure 1. DPR retrieves Wikipedia passages and BART generates text with a selected passage appended to its input. RAG-Sequence uses one latent document variable for the whole sequence, while RAG-Token uses one for each output position. Marginalization is restricted to the top k. Training updates the generator and query encoder, keeping the document index fixed. The authors discuss decoding variants for both models. [PDF, p. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.11401v4#page=1) [PDF, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.11401v4#page=2) [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.11401v4#page=3) [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.11401v4#page=4)
- Section 3. Open-domain questions use exact match, MS MARCO uses textual agreement, Jeopardy clues also receive human ratings, and FEVER uses label accuracy. Some baselines differ in their access to the supplied evidence. [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.11401v4#page=4) [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.11401v4#page=5) [PDF, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.11401v4#page=6)
- §§4.1–4.4, Tables 1–3 and Figure 2. On Natural Questions, RAG-Sequence scores 44.5 EM versus 41.5 for DPR. On standard TriviaQA, DPR scores 57.9 versus 56.8 for RAG-Sequence. The second TriviaQA column uses a different test. On FEVER, the top-10 hit rate for an article containing evidence is 90%, a separate measure from classification accuracy. Generation examples and the distribution of weights for Hemingway titles illustrate contributions from the two kinds of memory. [PDF, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.11401v4#page=6) [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.11401v4#page=7) [PDF, p. 18](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.11401v4#page=18)
- Section 4.5, Tables 4–6 and Figure 3. Across 452 Jeopardy pairs, raters preferred RAG-Token for factuality in 42.7% of cases and BART in 7.1%. The table gives 11.7% for “both good,” although the paragraph gives 17%. The table categories sum to 100%. Distinct trigrams make up 53.8% for RAG-Sequence versus 32.4% for BART on Jeopardy. Learned retrieval helps on NQ, but BM25 wins on FEVER development data. Changing the index date changes answers about leaders. The number of retrieved documents affects the variants differently. - §§5–6 and societal impact. The authors compare retrieval systems, memory models and generation with editing. They discuss knowledge updates and errors and biases in external documents. The references run through p. 16. - Appendices A–C and Figure 4. The open-domain QA experiments used 15 documents for Token and 50 for Sequence. Generation tasks used 10. Model order was randomized in the human-rating interface, and ratings from two unreliable evaluators were discarded. The authors describe Fairseq, FAISS, and training on eight V100 GPUs. [PDF, p. 17](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.11401v4#page=17)
- Appendices D–I and Table 7. The audit checked answer normalization, the two TriviaQA tests, the FEVER evaluation without evidence sentence extraction, unsuccessful empty-document variants and the parameter count. During preliminary story-generation training, the retriever sometimes selected the same documents repeatedly. The table lists 3,611 NQ test examples, 26,849 Jeopardy examples and 10,000 three-class FEVER examples.
