Sections 1–2 and Figures 1–4 extend the discussion from ordinary linearizability to persistence ordering in nonvolatile memory. After a crash, the state may retain some effects of calls that had not yet completed. The analysis assumes an unbuffered variant with synchronous flush instructions. Sections 5–6 and Appendix C. In the set implementation, validity and node marks carry persistent meaning. Links can be rebuilt by sorting. Sections C.1–6 formalize the key–value store, event order, node structure, state representation and invariants. C.7–10 combine the partial arguments. Lemma C.59 preserves the persistent representation because recovery changes links, then restores the volatile representation. Theorem C.60 relies on C.51 for omitted invocations, C.53 for the order of noncommuting operations and C.58 for correctness of the persistent state. These are three distinct proof obligations. C.11 and Figure 12 extend the argument to a contains operation that returns after traversing a previously deleted node. The paper includes illustrations and pseudocode without tables of measured results.
