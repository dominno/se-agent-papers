# Phantom Guardrails: When Self-Improving Agent Harnesses Fix Failures That Never Happened

Audit opened on 19 November 2026 for version `2607.13083v1`, 13 pages. [Paper record](../../sources/arxiv-2607.13083.md) · [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13083v1) · [Authors, license and checksum](../../raw/metadata/2607.13083.metadata.json)

## Sections and evidence

- **1–2. Question and background.** The authors ask whether the model proposes a repair when the recorded gameplay contains no defect. They distinguish this from improving a proxy metric at the expense of the actual outcome and from rejecting a valid action. [PDF, p. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.13083v1#page=1) [PDF, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.13083v1#page=2)
- **3. Experimental design.** MiniArena provides fixed run records and a deterministic check of whether the alleged violation exists. The metric counts activation of a specific safeguard for a violation class absent from the pool. The primary protocol uses five proposers, four subsamples and three random seeds per variant. [PDF, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.13083v1#page=2) [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.13083v1#page=3)
- **4.1–4.4. First proposal.** Table 1 and Figures 1–2 show unnecessary safeguards in 15/60 cases with legal moves that followed a familiar pattern, 0/60 without that pattern and 60/60 correct activations for actual violations. Two other instruction conditions each yielded 0/60. The authors give a 95% Wilson interval of 0.16–0.37 for 15/60. At 0/60 the upper limit is still about 0.06. [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.13083v1#page=3) [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.13083v1#page=4) [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.13083v1#page=5)
- **4.5, 5–6. Loop, discussion and conclusions.** When neutral additions were allowed, an unnecessary safeguard was added by round four in 11/60 runs. Requiring confirmation of the violation reduced this to 0/60. Figure 3 concerns a loop that retains accepted additions. The single environment and the concentration of cases in one model limit generalization. Section 6 and the references close the main text. [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.13083v1#page=5) [PDF, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.13083v1#page=6) [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.13083v1#page=7)
- **Appendices A–B.** GridErrand distinguishes presenting the same information differently from removing it. Tables 2–3 and Figure 4 vary the number of available examples and compare alternative descriptions. After controlling for sources of information, a comparison in a different environment yielded 12/60 versus 9/60, p = 0.47. Figure 5 shows how misleading information about utility can cause an effective component to be removed. [PDF, p. 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.13083v1#page=8) [PDF, p. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.13083v1#page=9)
- **Appendices C–G.** Appendix C documents instructions for a single attempt and for a loop. D gives the data composition and call counts. E reproduces a complete exchange for legal moves. F presents rationales selected by a predefined rule. G breaks down results by model, condition and safeguard type (Tables 4–5). ## Independent check of the paper record

The “fabric.” column of Table 4 gives 1 + 0 + 3 + 0 + 11 = 15 across the 60 primary trials. One model accounts for 11 cases. Adding three models from the supplementary analysis yields (15 + 2)/96 = 17.71%. Blank cells mark conditions that were never run. The earlier wording implied that all cases came from one model.
