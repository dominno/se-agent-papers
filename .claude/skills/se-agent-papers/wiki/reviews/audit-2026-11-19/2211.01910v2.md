## Sections 1–3. How the Instruction Is Created

APE treats natural language as a program space. A model proposes instructions from demonstrations by forward generation or infilling. Selection uses execution accuracy or the log probability of the correct answer. The algorithm can evaluate candidates on successive subsamples and generate similar variants, separating proposal quality from selection quality. ## Sections 4–6. What “human-level” means

In instruction induction, the reference is a human-written instruction executed by the model. The interquartile mean over 24 tasks is 0.810 for APE and 0.749 for human instructions. The authors reevaluated five tasks after changing hyperparameters. In v2, “human-level” denotes comparable performance, including small deficits such as −0.010 on Sentence Similarity. In the selected BIG-Bench subset, Table 6 yields 13 wins, four ties and four losses: 17 of 21 results matched or exceeded baseline. Losses include Logical Fallacy Detection and Ruin Names. ## Independent source check

The review confirmed that instructions are generated from demonstration pairs and ranked by two criteria. Across 24 induction tasks, the interquartile means differ by 0.810 − 0.749 = 0.061. An independent count of the signs of “APE minus Human” in the 21 BIG-Bench rows of Table 6 gives 13 positive differences, 4 zeros and 4 negative differences. In the separate comparison after hyperparameter changes, Table 17 reports deficits for pluralization (−0.004), sentence similarity (−0.010) and membership (−0.001). These scores measure how the evaluated model performs when executing the instructions.
