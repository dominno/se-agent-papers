# Finding Crash-Consistency Bugs with Bounded Black-Box Crash Testing

Independent audit on 19 November 2026 • 1810.02904v1 · [Paper record](../../sources/arxiv-1810.02904.md)

## Content and numbering of results

Sections 1–2 and Figure 1 define correct recovery after power loss. The criterion requires internal consistency and preservation of explicitly persisted data and metadata. Section 3 groups historical bug reports in Tables 1–2. Two bug families each occurred in two systems, giving 28 occurrences across 26 distinct families. Sections 4–5, Table 3 and Figures 2–5 distinguish ACE’s enumeration of small workloads from CrashMonkey’s evaluation of the recovered state. CrashMonkey compares the post-crash image with the expected state of files and directories explicitly persisted. The search is bounded by workload size, the file set and the crash points. The primitive-operation count excludes setup. Section 6 and Table 4. The experiment used 65 × 12 = 780 virtual machines. The times sum to 1 + 215 + 102 + 1,274 + 1,274 = 2,866 min, about 47.8 h, across 3.37 million workloads. Table 5 lists eight new btrfs bugs, two F2FS bugs and one FSCQ bug — 11 in all, of which the 10 in the title concern Linux file systems. The additional FSCQ case was traced to an unverified part of the implementation. The authors of the fix independently confirm that the optimization had no formal proof. Sections 7–8 compare B3 with model checking, fuzzing and formal verification. The 20.12 MB mean memory use for CrashMonkey applies to the sample measured. Restricting crashes to persistence points and using small initial images leaves other classes of states untested. ## Appendix 9

Section 9.1 lists 24 workloads for known bug families, two of which are each assigned to two file systems. This explains both the deduplicated count of 24 of 26 families and the 26 of 28 occurrences. Section 9.2 compares expected and observed states for 11 newly reported bugs, including one in FSCQ. Table 5 marks five entries as having a fix submitted by the time of publication.
