# DeepSWE: Measuring Frontier Coding Agents on Original, Long-Horizon Engineering Tasks

Audit opened on 19 November 2026 · version `2607.07946v1`. [Paper record](../../sources/arxiv-2607.07946.md) · [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07946v1) · [Retrieval metadata](../../raw/metadata/2607.07946.metadata.json)

## Review of sections and appendices

- Sections 1–3 and Figures 1–5. The authors constructed 113 new tasks across 91 repositories and five languages. A hidden test measures behavior, and the solutions were written independently of the public project histories. In the sample audit, agreement between a verifier and a second judge characterizes the measurement. Neither judge is established as infallible. - §§4–5 and Figure 6. Alongside repository selection and verifier construction, the authors describe a mini-swe-agent configuration with one tool and a shared instruction. A pilot compares native environments with a standardized environment on ten SWE-Bench Pro tasks. The target was four runs. Runs excluded for infrastructure failures were removed from both numerator and denominator and were not repeated. - Sections 6–7, Figures 7–10 and Table 1. Among the eight models with scores on both benchmarks, the spread is 69.8 percentage points on DeepSWE and 29.7 on SWE-Bench Pro. The authors also examine cost, time, token counts, agents writing their own tests and discrepancies in grading. For example, GPT-5.4 wrote tests in 85% versus 18% of trajectories. The tasks and prompts differed, so the comparison does not isolate the cause. - Sections 8–10. The choice of popular public repositories, a single evaluation environment and reliance on a model as judge limit generalization. The recalculated proportions are (2 + 8)/735 = 1.36% and (67 + 189)/789 = 32.45%. The authors report 1.4% and 32.4%. Both denominators count all audited runs, including for metrics labeled false positive or false negative. - Appendices A–E and Tables 2–5. Identifiers for individual runs link to the authors' artifact viewer. The review checked that the first cited trajectory was accessible. Further tables give configurations, the judge's taxonomy and examples of disagreement. The final example compares tasks that differ in specificity and in how much of the implementation they prescribe.
