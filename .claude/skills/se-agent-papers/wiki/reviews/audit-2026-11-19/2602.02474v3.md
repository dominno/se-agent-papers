## Sections 1–3. What the memory system learns

MemSkill separates a bank of memory operations, a controller that selects operations and an executor that updates memory. A designer analyzes representative failures and revises operation descriptions. Rollback limits regressions as measured by the training signal. The controller learns from reward, while the skill bank changes through edits to its text. ## Sections 4–5. Tables, transfer and ablation

For LLaMA-3.3-70B on LoCoMo, Table 1 reports 44.21 F1 and a judge score of 53.82. On ALFWorld with demonstrations, the two subsets average (77.14 + 83.58)/2 = 80.36% success. The lead over Mem0 in Table 1 is 80.36 − 77.82 = 2.54 percentage points. The +5.53 points in A.1 use LightMem from Table 4’s additional comparisons. Qwen does not improve on every metric. LongMemEval F1 is 29.19 for CoN and 25.29 for MemSkill. [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.02474v3#page=7) [PDF, p. 13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.02474v3#page=13)

LongMemEval and the Qwen configuration are labeled as transfer evaluations. HotpotQA tests a skill bank trained on LoCoMo with contexts of 50, 100, and 200 documents, comparing two methods. Ablations and the analysis of changes to skills relate the result to the choice of procedures, the number of operations selected, and how the bank evolves. ## Appendices A–F and conference disclosures

Appendix A adds ALFWorld baselines, runs without demonstrations, a separate AppWorld evaluation, a small model, stability, and comparisons with simplified variants. AppWorld reports the proportion of requirements passed and the number of steps. A gain on the first metric does not imply a gain on the second. Appendix B describes configuration, selection of hard examples, separation of experience from evaluation, and the PPO objective for sampling without replacement.
