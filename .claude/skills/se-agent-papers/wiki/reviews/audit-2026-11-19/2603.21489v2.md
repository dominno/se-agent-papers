Audit dated 19 November 2026, version `2603.21489v2`. [Publication card](../../sources/arxiv-2603.21489.md) · [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21489v2) · [Local provenance metadata](../../raw/metadata/2603.21489.metadata.json)

## Sections and appendices

- **Sections 1–2, Figure 1, and Table 1.** CAID uses a dependency graph, isolated working directories, parallel execution, and merging. A manager delegates available tasks and updates shared state as work finishes. [PDF, p. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.21489v2#page=1) · [PDF, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.21489v2#page=2) · [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.21489v2#page=3)
- **Section 3 and Table 2.** Commit0-Lite measures the proportion of a library’s tests that pass. PaperBench Code-Dev grades code production against a rubric. The study compares a single agent, CAID, and their combination, using two engineers on PaperBench and four on Commit0. The SDK, elapsed time, and cost appear in separate columns. [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.21489v2#page=4) · [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.21489v2#page=5)
- **Sections 4–7, Figures 2–5, and Table 3.** Isolation, engineer count, review, and verification of changes affect performance and elapsed time. Further parallelism produced only a small additional gain on PaperBench. Earlier verification increased elapsed time on the selected sample. Execution overhead, task selection, and the types of repository represented limit the findings. [PDF, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.21489v2#page=6) · [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.21489v2#page=7) · [PDF, p. 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.21489v2#page=8) · [PDF, p. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.21489v2#page=9) · [PDF, p. 10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.21489v2#page=10)
- **Appendices A–D, Tables 4–10, and Figure 6.** The audit checked delegation and dependency instructions, per-repository and per-paper rows, one-sided tests for three models, and the simpy trajectories. On Commit0-Lite, the gain for GLM 4.7 was 3.6 percentage points (*p* = 0.095), and the gain for MiniMax was 14.7 points (*p* = 0.007). ## Follow-up on MiniMax PaperBench results, 20 November 2026

**Table history.** Both arXiv source archives were examined. The file `tables/glm-paperbench-results.tex`, which includes Table 9 for MiniMax, is identical in v1 and v2 (SHA-256 `f9df13b35a4b750e7b4201b16f926a663155e8de5a7c87e0e89a4c8d669f980c`). All 20 pairs are written directly in LaTeX. They yield means of 8.125 and 8.950 and a one-sided paired test with 19 degrees of freedom and *t* = 0.23486. This reproduces the v1 row in `tables/stats.tex` (Δ = +0.8, *t* = 0.23, *p* = 0.408). Version 2 changes the row to +25.6, 5.27, and <0.0001. The COLM conference PDF still gives 0.4 and 42.0 for `lbcs` and a mean of 36.1 in Table 9. [Version 1 source archive](https://arxiv.org/src/2603.21489v1) · [Version 2 source archive](https://arxiv.org/src/2603.21489v2) · [Table 10 in v1, p. 19](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.21489v1#page=19) · [Table 10 in v2, p. 18](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.21489v2#page=18) · [COLM Table 9, p. 20](https://proceedings.colm.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/3b5c10bf5d70533e955cba9fba491bf4-Paper-Conference.pdf#page=20)

**Possible unit mismatch.** In nine consecutive rows, from `lbcs` to `sequential-neural-score-estimation`, the CAID score equals the combined score divided by 100 and rounded to one decimal place. Examples include 42.0 → 0.4 and 71.1 → 0.7. The means of the three time columns reproduce the summary after rounding (2,525.33, 3,042.40, and 5,567.73 seconds). The pattern suggests that the score columns mix fractions and percentage points. Multiplying every nonzero score below one by 100 gives means of 10.60 and 35.68, a gain of 25.08 points. Rounding has removed the precision needed to recover the original 20 pairs uniquely. [PDF v2, p. 22](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.21489v2#page=22)

**Search for supporting material.** Three revisions of the public repository contain evaluation code. The `.gitignore` excludes `outputs/`. The repository has one published branch and no releases. The accessible branches of public forks did not contain the original MiniMax grades. Issue #1 concerns Commit0 traces and includes no grading files. The Hugging Face paper page lists zero associated datasets. A search by arXiv ID on Zenodo and by title in OSF registrations found no data package. The public COLM page provides a PDF. A browser verification step limited access to the OpenReview discussion.
