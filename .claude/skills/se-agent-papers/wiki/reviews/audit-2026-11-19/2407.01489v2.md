## Section review

- Sections 1–2 compare repository-level issue resolution with earlier agents and fault localization. The authors prescribe the execution procedure. [PDF, p. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.01489v2#page=1) [PDF, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.01489v2#page=2) [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.01489v2#page=3) [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.01489v2#page=4)
- Section 3 and Figures 1–4. Localization narrows from files to classes and functions, then edit locations. The model generates patches for several location sets. Regression and reproduction tests filter candidates before voting. The figures illustrate localization and patch construction. [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.01489v2#page=5) [PDF, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.01489v2#page=6) [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.01489v2#page=7) [PDF, p. 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.01489v2#page=8) [PDF, p. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.01489v2#page=9)
- §§4–5, Tables 1–4 and Figures 5–7. GPT-4o resolved 96 of 300 Lite issues (32%) at an average cost of $0.70, ranking ninth when closed tools were included. The combined file-localization results contain the reference location in 81.67% of cases. With a budget of 40 patches, ten samples at each of four locations resolve 96 issues, versus 88 using a single location. Voting alone resolves 77 issues, regression filtering raises this to 81, and adding reproduction tests gives 96. Only 94 tests generated by the method recognized that the issue was resolved after the reference patch was applied. - §6, Tables 5–6 and Figures 8–9. The audit of issue descriptions examines hints about where to make changes, the quality of information and whether parts of a solution were already provided. On the 249-task Lite-S subset, the result was 84/249 = 33.73%. On Verified, it was 194/500 = 38.80%, below systems including Composio SWEkit with Claude. Some competing agents did better when location hints were absent.
