# ViBench 2.0: A Benchmark for Long-Horizon Software Building

Independent audit on 19 November 2026 • 2611.05620v1 · [Paper record](../../sources/arxiv-2611.05620.md)

## Section-by-section review

- **1–2, Figures 1–2.** The agent starts with an empty project and receives successive feature descriptions in one conversation. The solution must run on a fresh PostgreSQL database. Figure 2 shows feature dependencies in a transportation app. The 17 products have 241 stages and 544 plans, of which 463 combine features. The agent cannot see final test results while building. [PDF, p. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05620v1#page=1) [PDF, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05620v1#page=2) [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05620v1#page=3) [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05620v1#page=4)
- **3, protocol and metrics.** An external team built each of 17 products four times for each of nine models, totaling 612 attempts. An Opus 5.5 browser agent evaluated the plans. A product passed only if all graded plans passed. A separate metric reports the fraction of plans passing. Results were averaged first over builds of each product, then across products. [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05620v1#page=3) [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05620v1#page=4) [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05620v1#page=5) [PDF, p. 13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05620v1#page=13)
- **4, Tables 1–2.** Sol had the highest proportion of fully passing products (64.7%), and Astra scored highest on plans (98.0%). Astra passed the whole product in 60.3% of attempts and Opus in 48.5%. The 95% intervals for the top two pass@1 results overlap. Across releases of one model family on eight public products, Sol improved from 3.1% to 53.1%, while the build cost fell from $26.50 to $11.80. These figures cover application construction only. [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05620v1#page=5) [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05620v1#page=7)
- **5–6, restrictions and ethics.** Replit describes using the results for model selection, deployment checks and context decisions. The study evaluates functional compliance with tasks in a fixed order and no user feedback between stages. Nine products are private. The authors estimate about $1,440 in additional evaluation costs alone for a complete round. The judge was validated on earlier runs by the authors. [PDF, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05620v1#page=6) [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05620v1#page=7) [PDF, p. 13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05620v1#page=13)
- **Appendices A–C and Figure 3.** The review compares single-step tasks, multistage tasks and interface evaluations. The paper describes dependency ordering, containers, initial data and scoring. A pass on the first evaluation completes a plan. After a failure, evaluation can be repeated until three of at most five judgments agree. About 30 of 19,584 plans had no verdict and were excluded from the denominator. None changed the overall pass@1 in this series. [PDF, p. 11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05620v1#page=11) [PDF, p. 12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05620v1#page=12) [PDF, p. 13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05620v1#page=13)
- **Appendix D, Tables 3–4.** Of 477 cases originally marked as passing, the audit identified four false passes. In a separate experiment, 34 seeded defects × three trials produced 102 assessments. Six were excluded, including all three trials for a defect invisible in the interface. There were zero incorrect judgments in the 96 assessments containing a visible defect (95% CI 0–3.9%). The evidence therefore cannot establish the Table 4 heading’s literal claim that every one of 34 distinct defects was detected. - **Appendices E–F, Figures 4–5.** For the independent replications, the paper specifies the environment version, cost rules and editor differences. It reports both in-house and external results. Screenshots compare a file browser and a ride-booking flow. Visual aesthetics were excluded from scoring. [PDF, p. 14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05620v1#page=14) [PDF, p. 15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05620v1#page=15) [PDF, p. 16](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05620v1#page=16)
- **Appendices G–H, Tables 5–7.** The tables break down results by public product and hidden subset. All 36 independent attempts at the Figma-inspired product failed, although one reference implementation in Figure 4 passes every plan. In a separate ViBench 1.0 replication on 45 tasks, the highest pass@1 was 88.9%. ## Independent verification

For Sol, the eight public tasks in Table 5 yield 2 + 4 + 2 + 0 + 0 + 1 + 4 + 4 = 17 successes in 32 builds. On the hidden subset, 75% of 36 is 27 successes. Together, 44/68 = 64.71%, reproducing Table 1. For the judge in Table 4, the category denominators sum to 15 + 15 + 15 + 14 + 10 + 27 = 96, consistent with the exclusions in Appendix D.
