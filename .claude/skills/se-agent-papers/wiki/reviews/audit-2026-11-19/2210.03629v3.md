# ReAct: Synergizing Reasoning and Acting in Language Models

## Review of sections and appendices

- Sections 1–2 and Figure 1. A sequence of reasoning, action, and observation lets the agent revise its plan in response to search results or changes in its environment. The frequency of reasoning steps depends on the task. [PDF, p. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.03629v3#page=1) [PDF, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.03629v3#page=2) [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.03629v3#page=3)
- §3, Tables 1–2 and Figure 2. HotpotQA uses exact match and FEVER uses classification accuracy. ReAct scores 27.4 and 60.9, respectively. Chain-of-thought with self-consistency scores 33.4 and 60.4. Switching from ReAct to CoT-SC gives 35.1 on HotpotQA. Manual analysis of trajectories distinguishes evidence retrieval, hallucination and faulty reasoning. [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.03629v3#page=4) [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.03629v3#page=5) [PDF, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.03629v3#page=6)
- §4, Tables 3–4 and Figure 3. On ALFWorld, the best of six demonstration sets achieved 71% success, compared with 37% for the best of eight BUTLER variants. ReAct averaged 57%. On WebShop, ReAct's 40.0% success rate exceeds IL's 29.1% by 10.9 percentage points and IL+RL's 28.7% by 11.3 points. The comparison that yields 9.9 points is with Act (30.1%). [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.03629v3#page=7) [PDF, p. 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.03629v3#page=8)
- §§5–6. The authors relate the method to conditioning models on demonstrations and interacting with the environment. Execution incurs search costs and is subject to retrieval errors. A knowledge-update example also shows how the world can change after the reference label was assigned. [PDF, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.03629v3#page=6) [PDF, p. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.03629v3#page=9) [PDF, p. 14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.03629v3#page=14)
- Appendices A–B, Table 5 and Figures 4–5. GPT-3 was evaluated on 500 HotpotQA questions and 134 ALFWorld tasks using demonstrations selected on PaLM. Examples show human intervention and wording differences. Further details cover fine-tuning and the instruction variant. [PDF, p. 14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.03629v3#page=14) [PDF, p. 15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.03629v3#page=15)
- Appendices C–E and Tables 6–10. Prompts and example trajectories for HotpotQA, FEVER, WebShop, and ALFWorld document permitted actions and how observations were used. The final examples distinguish a correct answer with an incorrect rationale, a retrieval error, and an ambiguous label.
