# Darwin Gödel Machine: Open-Ended Evolution of Self-Improving Agents

Audit opened on 19 November 2026 · version `2505.22954v3`. [Paper record](../../sources/arxiv-2505.22954.md) · [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22954v3) · [Retrieval metadata](../../raw/metadata/2505.22954.metadata.json)

## Review of sections and appendices

- Sections 1–3 and Figure 1. The Darwin Gödel Machine creates successive agents, selecting a parent from its archive and using a model to modify its code. Tests serve in place of a formal proof of improvement. The designers fix the selection and archive-maintenance rules. - Section 4, Figures 2–4. After 80 iterations on a 200-task SWE-bench subset, the score rose from 20.0% to 50.0%. On 50 selected Polyglot tasks it rose from 14.0% to 38.0%. On full Polyglot it rose from 14.2% to 30.7%. The gains are 24.0 percentage points on the selection sample and 16.5 on the full set. The full set includes the selection tasks and is only partly held out. DGM uses pass@1, whereas the original Polyglot leaderboard usually uses pass@2. - Sections 5–6, ethics, and reproducibility. The environment, logging, and oversight constrained the actions available. The authors discuss the mismatch between reward and desired behavior and the risks of transferring these methods beyond a supervised benchmark. The experiment does not demonstrate unlimited improvement. [PDF, p. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.22954v3#page=9) [PDF, p. 10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.22954v3#page=10) [PDF, p. 11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.22954v3#page=11)
- Appendices A–B, Figures 5–7 and Tables 1–2. On the same SWE-bench tasks, the baseline without archive exploration scored 23%, the variant without self-improvement scored 39% and the full DGM scored 50%. Of newly generated agents, 51.3% retained basic editing functionality with DGM, compared with 32.5% in each control. The appendices cover transfer across models, repeated runs and related work. - Appendices C–E, Algorithms 1–3 and Table 3. The appendices compare the initial agent, the parent selection method, three experimental arrangements and the model parameters. Budgets differed: a DGM run on SWE-bench was estimated at about $22,000, compared with about $10,000 for an individual control. Appendix E lists the tasks and compares results on matching subsets. A case study of false completion claims and the safety discussion motivate checking observations and evaluations independently of the agent. The conclusion considers changes to the selection procedure. The public repository contains code, tests and data for analysis.
