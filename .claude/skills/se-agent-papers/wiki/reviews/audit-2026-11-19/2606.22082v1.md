# CodeTeam: An LLM-Powered Multi-Agent Framework for Repository-Level Code Generation

Audit opened on 19 November 2026 · version `2606.22082v1`. [Paper record](../../sources/arxiv-2606.22082.md) · [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22082v1) · [Retrieval metadata](../../raw/metadata/2606.22082.metadata.json)

## Section review

- Sections 1–3, Figure 1, Listing 1, Algorithm 1 and Table 1. Architect agents propose designs, a CTO agent selects the contract, and developers are assigned files in an order determined by interface dependencies. QA initiates repairs. The authors separately evaluate a prompt-based variant and a supervised fine-tuned variant under common generation rules. They specify the ablations and the different metrics used for the two benchmarks. Mean SketchBLEU rises from 47.6 ± 2.2 for CodeS PE to 51.7 ± 1.8 for CodeTeam PE, and from 58.0 ± 1.6 to 60.9 ± 1.4 after fine-tuning. The results include similarity components, sizes, and wins by repository. - Tables 5–6. Across 104 NL2Repo-Bench tasks, the mean within-repository test pass rate is 34.6 ± 1.3% for PE and 42.3 ± 1.1% for SFT. Pass@1 for complete solutions is 5.1% and 6.1%, respectively. In the 30-task sample, packaging and import errors account for 22.1% + 19.4% = 41.5% of classified CodeTeam PE failures. This row is an exception to the claim that such failures constitute a majority for every method. - Tables 7–9 and Figures 4–5. The ablations cover planning with retrieved patterns, competition between proposals, allocation, and synchronization. Disabling dynamic allocation increases mean QA rounds from 3.6 to 5.2 and mean interface mismatches from 7.8 to 12.4.
