## Review of sections and appendices

- §§1–2, Tables 1–2 and Figures 1–2. The environment restores system state and evaluates final artifacts with a script. The agent observes a desktop screenshot, an accessibility tree or both. Its action space and termination conditions differ from text generation. - Section 3, Tables 3–4, and Figures 3–4. There are 369 Ubuntu tasks, comprising 268 within a single application and 101 spanning multiple applications. The authors also give 43 Windows tasks. For Ubuntu they describe 302 initial states and 134 evaluation scripts. The distribution of completion times and the human success rate are separate measurements. [PDF, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.07972v2#page=6) [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.07972v2#page=7) [PDF, p. 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.07972v2#page=8) [PDF, p. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.07972v2#page=9)
- Sections 4–5, Tables 5–7 and Figures 5–10. GPT-4 with an accessibility tree achieves the best model result in Table 5 (12.24%). The human score is 72.36%, a difference of 60.12 percentage points. On 28 selected tasks, changing the window size lowers the score from 50.79% to 15.04%. The analyses cover trajectory history, resolution, perturbations, cross-application tasks and recognition of interface elements. The appendices supplement the aggregate score with exact agent parameters, tree filtering, on-screen markers, full per-application results and examples of successful and failed action sequences.
