# RAPTOR: Recursive Abstractive Processing for Tree-Organized Retrieval

Review of version `2401.18059v1` • 19 November 2026 • 23 pages. [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.18059v1) · [Metadata and license](../../raw/metadata/2401.18059.metadata.json) · [Paper record](../../sources/arxiv-2401.18059.md)

## Review of sections and tables

- Sections 1–3. To support questions that require more than one passage, the authors cluster text embeddings and recursively summarize the clusters (Figure 1). Retrieval can start at the root or compare nodes across all levels together (Figure 2). Figure 3 compares the variants on 20 QASPER documents. The authors selected the all-level search with a 2,000-token limit, reduced to 400 for UnifiedQA. Table 8 and Figure 4 illustrate which tree layers contribute to answering particular questions. ## Independent verification

On QuALITY-dev with the same GPT-3 model, the differences are 62.4 − 60.4 = 2.0 and 62.4 − 57.3 = 5.1 percentage points. The larger 21.5-point difference on HARD compares two complete systems. Searching all levels performed best on three of the five stories in Tables 8 and 14–17. On story 2 it scored 47.1%, below the 64.7% for two levels. On story 4, leaf-only search performed best (94.7%).
