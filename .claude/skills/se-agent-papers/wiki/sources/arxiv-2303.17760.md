# CAMEL: Communicative Agents for "Mind" Exploration of Large Language Model Society

**Paper:** [2303.17760](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17760) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.17760) · [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.17760)  
**Review status:** `fulltext_reviewed` · **Full text reviewed:** **yes**

## Research findings

**Method.** After refining the task, models assigned the user and assistant roles conduct a dialogue. The authors assess preferences for dialogue summaries and separately evaluate the coding performance of models fine-tuned on synthetic data. [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.17760v2#page=4) [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.17760v2#page=5) [PDF, p. 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.17760v2#page=8) [PDF, p. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.17760v2#page=9) [PDF, p. 10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.17760v2#page=10)

**Key result.** Of 453 votes on AI Society, 76.3% favored CAMEL, 10.4% favored a single GPT-3.5-turbo response and 13.3% were ties. In the separate HumanEval test, CAMEL-7B achieved 14.0% pass@1, versus 10.5% for the base LLaMA-7B. [PDF, p. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.17760v2#page=9) [PDF, p. 10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.17760v2#page=10)

**Limitations and scope.** Voting measured preference for answers summarized by GPT-4. Human evaluation covered AI Society alone and may have been influenced by answer length. The dialogues included role reversals and interruptions on reaching the token limit. ## Overview

The study explores autonomous agent collaboration through role-playing and prompts that keep it aligned with human goals. [PDF, p. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.17760v2#page=1)
