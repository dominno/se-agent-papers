# Generative Agents: Interactive Simulacra of Human Behavior

**Paper:** [2304.03442](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03442) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.03442) · [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442)  
**Review status:** `fulltext_reviewed` · **Full text reviewed:** **yes**

## Research findings

**Method.** Smallville contained 25 agents with memory, reflection and planning. In a separate controlled evaluation, 100 people ranked responses to questions about a randomly selected agent. [PDF, p. 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.03442v2#page=8) [PDF, p. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.03442v2#page=9) [PDF, p. 13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.03442v2#page=13) [PDF, p. 14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.03442v2#page=14)

**Key result.** The full architecture scored 29.89 in TrueSkill, the variant without reflection scored 26.88, and removing planning as well reduced the score to 25.64. After two simulated days, 13 of 25 agents knew of the party, including its organizer. Five of the 12 invitees attended. [PDF, p. 14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.03442v2#page=14) [PDF, p. 16](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.03442v2#page=16)

**Limitations and scope.** The ranking concerns how believable the responses appeared to an observer. The ablations removed components successively. Message spread and coordination were measured in one world over two game days.
