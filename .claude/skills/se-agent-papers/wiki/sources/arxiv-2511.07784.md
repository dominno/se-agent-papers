# Can LLM Agents Really Debate? A Controlled Study of Multi-Agent Debate in Logical Reasoning

**Paper:** [2511.07784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07784) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07784) · [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07784)  
**Review status:** `fulltext_reviewed` · **Full text reviewed:** **yes**

## Research findings

**Method.** The pool contained 1,800 Knight–Knave–Spy puzzles with six varied parameters. Agents first proposed roles independently, then debated and voted. The experiments used 100 games per configuration. [PDF, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07784v1#page=3) [PDF, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07784v1#page=4) [PDF, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07784v1#page=5) [PDF, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07784v1#page=6)

**Key result.** In a regression of the final proportion of correctly identified roles on 745 observations, the coefficient was 0.600 (SE 0.100) for initial accuracy and 0.066 (SE 0.014) for agent count. The model explained 39.3% of the variance. [PDF, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07784v1#page=7)

**Limitations and scope.** The experiment increased team size by adding the strongest agent. Prescribed roles, a staged debate and the limited choice of models restrict how far the findings generalize to creative tasks with multiple solutions.
